# IDG Mapping Findings

## 1. Scope and Method

Three landmark DT leadership papers were mapped item-by-item onto the 23 IDG skills across 5 dimensions. The mapping was conducted at sub-element level — not cluster-to-dimension, but individual competency/behavior to IDG skill — to make convergences and gaps precise.

| Paper | Type | Unit of analysis | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Philip, Gilli & Knappstein (2023) | Empirical Delphi (18 senior managers, 3 rounds) | 27 behavioral competency items across 4 clusters | `philip2023_idg_mapping.csv` |
| Schiuma, Schettini, Santarsiero & Carlucci (2022) | Conceptual literature synthesis (3 leadership streams) | 6 competencies × multiple sub-elements | `schiuma2022_idg_mapping.csv` |
| Müller, Konzag, Nielsen & Sandholt (2024) | Systematic literature review (67 papers) + theoretical framework | 4 archetypical portfolios × 3 competency categories each | `muller2024_idg_mapping.csv` |

## 2. IDG Coverage by Dimension

### 2.1 Being — Relationship to Self

| IDG Skill | Philip (2023) | Schiuma (2022) | Müller (2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inner compass | Creating Purpose; Passion | C2: Higher purpose/societal good; C3: Shared sense of purpose | Bricoleur: Technology-culture fit (protecting organisational values); Competitor: Strategic leadership anchored in culture |
| Integrity and Authenticity | Instilling Trust; Honesty | C2: Ethical/values-driven decisions; C6: Leading by example | Organizer: Building trust (being honest, consistent and fair) |
| Openness and Learning mindset | Leading in Uncertain Times; Openness; Curiosity; Flexibility | C3: Open learning environments; C6: Continual selfless learning | Challenger: Risk-taking and uncertainty embracing; Fail-fast learning from experimentation |
| Self-awareness | Self-Awareness | C2: Emotional intelligence; working on oneself | — (absent) |
| Presence | Leading in Uncertain Times; Listening; Resilience | C1: Intuition and tacit knowledge; C3: Well-being as foundation | Organizer: Creating positive attentive work environment |

**Finding:** Philip and Schiuma fully cover the Being dimension; Müller covers it partially. Müller's SLR coded from existing papers that use functional language — the introspective capacity of Self-awareness does not appear in any of the 67 papers reviewed, making it the starkest evidence of a missing inner-development frame in the DT leadership literature. Philip's practitioner open-text quotes — self-care, mindfulness, yoga, burnout risk — align directly with Presence and Self-awareness but are not formalised as Delphi items, because practitioners have no taxonomy to express them.

|---|---|---|---|

| Perspective skills | Customer Centricity; Systems Thinking | C1: Holistic business perspective; C5: Understanding diverse viewpoints | Challenger: Insight into adoption experiences across settings; Competitor: Outside-in industry perspective |
| Sense-making | Holistic Thinking; Problem Solving | C1: Processing fast-paced data; C5: Dialectical synthesis | Challenger: Scenario planning and vision coherence; Bricoleur: Recognising transformation potential through patterns; Competitor: Data-driven insight extraction |

**Finding:** All three papers fully cover the Thinking dimension and it is the most consistently mapped dimension across all sources. Complexity awareness is the most densely mapped IDG skill overall — appearing in all three papers under different labels (Systems Thinking, dialectical thinking, technology-landscape analysis). Philip's practitioners call systems thinking "currently still a very academic understanding"; Schiuma reframes it as dialectical thinking; Müller distributes it across all four portfolios as a context-specific competency. IDG's Complexity awareness is the practitioner-facing unifying label.

### 2.3 Relating — Caring for Others and the World

| IDG Skill | Philip (2023) | Schiuma (2022) | Müller (2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appreciation | — | C6: Psychological safety; non-intimidating atmosphere | Organizer: Creating positive inclusive team environment |
| Connectedness | Team Orientation | C2: Beyond-profit societal thinking; C4: Building shared identity | — (absent) |
| Humility | — | C6: Non-threatening leadership style | Organizer: Coaching rather than directing; granting autonomy |
| Empathy and Compassion | Empathy; Social Competence; Customer Centricity | C2: EI and well-being; C4: Empathetic storytelling; C5: Listening; C6: Caring for growth | Organizer: Coaching to promote development; Competitor: Customer-oriented empathy; understanding customer needs |

**Finding:** Relating is the most fragmented dimension across all three papers. Philip validates Empathy empirically but leaves Appreciation, Humility and Connectedness absent. Müller partially recovers Appreciation and Humility through the Organizer portfolio but only in functional terms (positive environment, coaching style) — not as inner qualities. Connectedness is entirely absent from Müller: the SLR describes *managing* stakeholders but not *feeling part of* a larger whole, which is the IDG distinction. Schiuma is the only paper that covers all four Relating skills, grounded in the philosophical wise/transformative leadership tradition. The gap between Philip's practitioner quote (*"Empathy — often lacking, been driven out of people through outdated focus on results"*) and its absence as a formal Delphi item illustrates the problem your artifact addresses.

| IDG Skill | Philip (2023) | Schiuma (2022) | Müller (2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Communication skills | Listening; Influencing; Knowledge Sharing; Relationship-Building; Communication; Presentation; Social Competence | C4: Storytelling/rhetoric; logos-pathos-ethos; C5: Empathetic dialogue | Organizer: Mastering digital communication platforms; Bricoleur: Convincing employees and visualising benefits; Challenger: Vision presentation |
| Co-creation skills | Enabling People; Knowledge Sharing; Co-Creation; Team Orientation | C3: Creating BA/knowledge spaces; C5: Mobilizing diverse groups; C6: Empowering others | Bricoleur: Knowledge consolidation and brokering; Organizer: Team collaboration and inclusion; Competitor: Product co-creation with stakeholders |
| Inclusive mindset and Intercultural competence | — (weakly: Social Competence) | C3: Open stakeholder networks; C5: Engaging cross-cultural teams | Bricoleur: Brokering across organisational boundaries; Organizer: Including all stakeholders across diverse groups |
| Trust | Instilling Trust; Relationship-Building | C4: Ethos and credibility; C6: Trust-based empowerment | Organizer: Honest consistent fair leadership; Bricoleur: Culture-aligned transformation |

**Finding:** Collaborating is the most richly and consistently mapped dimension across all three papers. Communication skills and Co-creation skills receive mappings from every paper and every portfolio — confirming that DT leadership is fundamentally a collaborative act, not a solo technical skill. Inclusive mindset remains the structural gap in Philip (entirely absent), but both Schiuma and Müller address it, making it an empirically motivated addition your artifact provides. Müller adds an important nuance: the same IDG skill appears across multiple portfolios with different operational flavors (e.g., Co-creation skills = knowledge brokering in the Bricoleur, team collaboration in the Organizer, product development in the Competitor).

### 2.5 Acting — Driving Change

| IDG Skill | Philip (2023) | Schiuma (2022) | Müller (2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Courage | Courage; Proactiveness | C1: Decisiveness under uncertainty; C5: Charismatic energy; positive disobedience | Challenger: Risk-taking and status-quo challenging; Entrepreneurial spirit and experimentation |
| Creativity | Proactiveness; Problem Solving | C3: Facilitating creative collisions; C4: Imagination and vision; C5: Contradiction as innovation; C6: Encouraging initiative | Challenger: Experimenting with undemonstrated technologies; Competitor: Developing new product/service opportunities |
| Optimism | — | C2: Nurturing optimism, idealism, hopefulness | — (absent) |
| Perseverance | Resilience | C3: Demonstrating commitment; C5: Sustained energy; C6: Selfless continual learning | Challenger: Fail-fast discipline; Bricoleur: Project-by-project sustained execution |

**Finding:** Courage and Creativity are consistently confirmed across all three papers, making them the two most empirically robust Acting competencies for DT leadership. Optimism is the sharpest white space: present only in Schiuma (grounded in the idealist/righteous leader archetype), absent in both Philip's Delphi and Müller's SLR. Philip and Müller frame Acting reactively — resilience under VUCA, risk tolerance, fail-fast recovery; Schiuma frames it generatively — optimism and creativity as engines of new futures. This framing difference is consequential for an IDG-based artifact: the IDG Acting dimension is about *driving* change, not merely surviving it.

## 4. Key Analytical Points for the Paper

**4.1 Convergent validity across three independent methods**
Three papers using three different methods — empirical Delphi, conceptual synthesis, and systematic literature review — independently surface competencies that map onto all five IDG dimensions. No paper used the IDG framework. This cross-methodological convergence is the core argument: IDG names and structures what the DT leadership literature already recognises but cannot articulate coherently.

**4.2 The Being dimension is underrepresented in literature, overrepresented in practitioner quotes**
Müller's 67-paper SLR produces zero entries for Self-awareness. Philip's formal Delphi items underrepresent Being-level qualities, yet the open-text practitioner quotes — self-care, mindfulness, yoga, burnout risk, emotional intelligence — are densely Being-aligned. The gap is not in what practitioners need; it is in what the literature has formalised. IDG fills exactly this formalisation gap.

**4.3 The three papers are epistemologically complementary**
- Philip: *empirical legitimacy* — practitioners independently validate the competency space
- Schiuma: *philosophical depth* — phronesis and wise leadership ground the inner-development logic
- Müller: *contextual nuance* — the contingency finding shows people-oriented competencies are universally needed across all four portfolios regardless of context

Together they triangulate the same competency space from different epistemological directions. IDG provides the unifying taxonomy.

**4.4 Müller's contingency finding is an argument for IDG, not against it**
Müller argues there is no one-size-fits-all competency set — different portfolios suit different contexts. However, people-oriented competencies appear in all four portfolios. The IDG framework, as a taxonomy of inner capacities underlying people-oriented competencies, is therefore the most context-invariant foundation available. Müller shows *which* outer behaviors vary by context; IDG addresses the *inner* capacities that enable any of those behaviors.

**4.5 Complexity awareness is the most cross-validated IDG skill**
It appears under five different labels across the three papers: Systems Thinking (Philip), dialectical thinking (Schiuma C5), technology-landscape analysis (Müller Challenger), incremental transformation pattern recognition (Müller Bricoleur), and competitive dynamics analysis (Müller Competitor). This breadth of surface expression with a single underlying IDG skill demonstrates the taxonomic value of the IDG framework over ad-hoc competency labelling.

**4.6 The Relating dimension is the most culturally contingent and most under-theorised**
Philip explicitly notes communal cultures (Africa, Asia) prioritize collaboration; Western consulting firms prioritize individual skills. Relating skills — Appreciation, Humility, Connectedness — are precisely those most shaped by cultural background. Yet they receive the least systematic coverage across all three papers. An IDG-grounded artifact must make these skills visible and developable without prescribing a single cultural expression.
