## 1. Executive Summary
The research corpus confirms a well-established but fragmented body of knowledge on leadership competencies in digital transformation. Three scholarly communities address the research question from different angles: (1) a digital leadership competency community identifying what competencies DT leaders need and how to measure them; (2) a mindset and inner skills community examining how psychological capacities (growth mindset, emotional intelligence, paradox mindset) enable digital change; and (3) an emergent Inner Development Goals (IDG) community pioneering a structured inner-capacity framework aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals.
The critical gap is the missing bridge between these three communities. Despite 208-citation landmark work on transformative leadership competencies (Schiuma 2022/2024) and a growing IDG movement with dedicated assessment tools and practice volumes, no study has applied the IDG taxonomy — Being, Thinking, Relating, Collaborating, Acting — as a structured organizing principle for a technical leadership competency framework in digital transformation contexts.
Furthermore, Design Science Research (DSR) has been applied to digital mindset model development (Kocak 2026; Blanka 2022) but not to a holistic IDG-grounded competency framework for technical leaders. Shtaltovna (2025) is the only paper connecting all three constructs (IDG + digital transformation + competency), confirming the novelty of the proposed integration. This gap directly justifies the proposed DSR artifact.

## 2. Thematic Analysis
### Theme 1: Digital Transformation Leadership Competency Frameworks
- Core Focus: What specific competencies do leaders need to drive digital transformation, and how can these be structured into validated, prescriptive models?
- Schiuma et al. (2022, 2024): The two most-cited contributions (208 and 73 citations). The 'transformative leadership compass' defines 6 competencies: meaning-making, people orchestration, digital fluency, learning agility, ambiguity navigation, and customer centricity. Dominant prescriptive model in the field but does NOT integrate IDG dimensions — direct gap for the proposed artifact to fill.
- Müller et al. (2024) — 76 cit: Contingency approach showing DT leadership competency requirements vary by organizational context. Critical design constraint for the proposed artifact's scope conditions.
- Philip et al. (2023) — 42 cit: Cross-sectoral Delphi study identifying key DT leadership competencies validated by global managers. Among the most methodologically rigorous empirical sources.
- Weber et al. (2022) — 45 cit: Validated DT leadership framework distinguishing strategic, enabling, and operational leadership roles — provides structural template.
- Gilli et al. (2023) — 37 cit: Job advertisement mining of 140 DT postings bridging academic models with market reality. Applicable as secondary validation method.
- Bouwmans et al. (2024) — 17 cit: SLR to develop a DT skills framework — the closest prior SLR to the proposed study's methodology. Key predecessor.
- Key Papers: Schiuma et al. (2022); Müller et al. (2024); Philip et al. (2023)
### Theme 2: Inner Skills, Mindset & Emotional Intelligence in Digital Leadership

- Poláková et al. (2023) — 218 cit: Soft skills in Industry 5.0 — highest citation paper overall. Confirms human-centric inner capabilities are increasingly central as technology becomes more autonomous.

- Kocak (2026): Applies DSR to design and validate a digital mindset model. Direct methodological precedent for the proposed artifact.

- Key Papers: Blanka et al. (2022); Poláková et al. (2023); Ertiö et al. (2024)
### Theme 3: Inner Development Goals (IDG) — Theory, Measurement & Leadership Development
- Core Focus: The IDG framework as structured taxonomy of inner capacities for sustainability-oriented leadership, with emerging tools for measurement, education, and institutionalization.
- IDG Volumes (Suarez/Egel 2025, Vols 1 & 2): Most comprehensive IDG synthesis covering all five dimensions — Being, Thinking, Relating, Collaborating, Acting — with global case studies. Foundational references for IDG taxonomy.
- Rhodes et al. (2025): Measurement and training of IDGs — how to operationalize and assess IDG dimensions in organizational leadership contexts.

- Wamsler et al. (2025): IDG institutionalization — how IDGs scale from individual development to cultural/systemic transformation. Critical for embedding the proposed framework in organizations.
- Egel et al. (2025): Integrated framework of Global Leadership for Sustainability (GLfS) + IDGs — most directly relevant IDG+leadership integration.
- Shtaltovna (2025): ONLY paper connecting all three constructs: EI + digital literacy + IDG + employability. Confirms novelty of the proposed integration.
- Satterwhite et al. (2025): Systems Thinking + IDGs connection — validates systems thinking as both an IDG 'Thinking' sub-dimension and measurable leadership capability.
- Key Papers: Egel et al. (2025); Rhodes et al. (2025); Shtaltovna (2025)

## 3. Methodology & Design Trends
- Delphi Studies: Philip et al. (2023), Müller et al. (2024) — validate competency lists across expert panels. Directly applicable for Phase 3 (expert validation) of proposed study.
- Scale Development (Survey): Munsamy (2023); Abbu (2023/2025) — psychometric rigor. Provides validated instruments but limited prescriptive design guidance.
- Job Advertisement Mining / Content Analysis: Gilli (2023) — bridges theoretical frameworks and market expectations. Applicable as secondary validation method.
- Design Science Research (DSR): Blanka et al. (2022), Kocak (2024/2026) — most closely aligned with proposed method. CRITICAL precedent showing DSR is applicable to competency model artifacts.
- Systematic Literature Review (SLR): Bouwmans (2024), Rakovic (2024), Ngayo Fotso (2021) — methodological templates for the proposed knowledge base phase.
- Common Evaluation Metrics: Competency relevance rankings (Delphi, Likert); Scale reliability (Cronbach's α); Adoption/performance outcomes; Framework completeness (expert panels); IDG assessment via Sustainability Mindset Indicator (Rimanoczy 2025).

## 4. Identified Gaps & Limitations
- No IDG-grounded competency framework for technical leaders in DT: The IDG framework has never been applied as the organizing taxonomy for a technical leadership competency framework in DT. This is the central gap.
- DT leadership frameworks lack inner-skills depth: Existing frameworks (Schiuma 2022/2024; Müller 2024; Weber 2022) identify *which* competencies are needed but not *how* inner capacities (being, presence, systems thinking) enable those competencies. The mechanistic link is missing.
- IDG research does not address digital transformation contexts: The IDG literature covers sustainability, education, and general leadership — but no study operationalizes IDG dimensions for technical leadership roles (CTO, IT manager, digital transformation lead).
- DSR is underutilized for competency framework design: Only Blanka (2022) and Kocak (2026) use DSR for competency artifacts. Prescriptive design of competency frameworks as IS artifacts has significant untapped potential.
- No integrated IDG + DT leadership measurement tool: Separate tools exist (Rimanoczy 2025 for IDG; Munsamy 2023 / Abbu 2025 for digital leadership) but no instrument measures IDG-aligned competencies in DT leadership contexts.
- Technical leadership roles are underspecified: Most frameworks address 'digital leaders' or 'managers' generally. CTO, IT manager, and software architect roles have distinct competency demands that are rarely targeted.
- Shtaltovna (2025) is the only paper connecting IDG + DT + competency: Confirms the proposed integration is novel and constitutes a clear research contribution.
- No prior SLR used IDG as an organizing lens: Bouwmans (2024) and Rakovic (2024) did not map literature through the IDG framework — providing a clear methodological contribution.
