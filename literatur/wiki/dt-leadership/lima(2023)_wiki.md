## 2. Overview

The paper addresses Lean project management (LPM) soft skills in the context of the fourth industrial revolution (Industry 4.0). It states: "This paper aims to identify the critical soft skills (CSSs) to implement LPM in the I4.0 era and to investigate, from the perspective of organizations, the latent factors for LPM implementation that generate sustainable value." [Page 1]

The methodological approach is described as: "a mixed-method approach was used, combining literature review and a survey with 166 Brazilian professionals." [Page 1]

Stated findings: "Research findings revealed convergences regarding the practitioners' perception regarding the ten CSSs identified in the literature, and portray two latent factors, human and process factors, which can assist decision-makers in implementing LPM by offering a better perspective of the key factors that add sustainable value for companies in the digital transformation context." [Page 1]

### Research Questions
- **RQ1:** "What are the CSSs for implementing LPM in the digital transformation era?" [Page 3]
- **RQ2:** "From the organizations' perspective, what are the latent factors for LPM implementation that generate sustainable value in the digital transformation era?" [Page 3]

### Goals
The goals are stated as: "(i) to identify the CSSs for implementing LPM in the digital transformation era and (ii) to obtain the latent factors that order themselves into a framework to facilitate the socio-technical implementation of Lean, adding sustainable value in project management from the perspective of project managers in a developing country." [Page 3]

### Stated Novelty
"the novelty of this work is due to a proposal of a managerial artifact that combines CSS-based LPM, digital transformation, and sustainable value through an empirical study in a developing country." [Page 3]

## 3. Core Concepts and Definitions

### Project (PMBOK)
"a project is 'a temporary effort undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result' that has a defined beginning and end." [Page 3]

### Lean Project Management (LPM)
"The Lean project management (LPM) approach emphasizes added value, decreases waste and unproductivity, and helps control project risks, costs, complexity, and time." [Page 2]; also stated identically [Page 4]

LPM is further described as: "LPM can also be viewed as a set of technical and cultural tools that helps the organization attain higher operational efficiency." [Page 2]

Per a cited source: "to achieve such goals, LPM considers all of the people and processes involved in the project and simplifies them to meet the stakeholders' needs in the shortest possible time, minimizing the stress of those involved." [Page 2]; with the dictum "any project worth doing is worth doing quickly". [Page 2]

"LPM is a combination of continuous improvement tools and socio-compartmental abilities, and to be effective it must be adopted from the shop floor to executive management." [Page 4]

### Lean Projects
"projects are temporary systems, and when these systems are structured to deliver products while maximizing their value and minimizing waste, they are considered Lean projects." [Page 4]

### Soft Skills and Critical Soft Skills (CSSs)
Soft skills: "personal attributes that enable someone to interact effectively and harmoniously with other people and shape how flexible and cooperative a person can be, directly affecting their ability to solve problems and make decisions in specific contexts." [Page 5]

Critical Soft Skills: "Even among soft skills, some can be more relevant than others, depending on the context. These are then defined as Critical Soft Skills (CSS)." [Page 5]

### VUCA
"in today's VUCA (volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous) world, the proposed socio-technical framework for LPM, based on CSSs for I4.0 (e.g., agile mindset), can lead to the successful and sustainable delivery of value in projects." [Page 3]

### Gemba
"Gemba is the Japanese word for the 'real place' where things happen. Going to the Gemba is an allusion to the 'go-see' principle, which consists of leaving your workplace and seeing for yourself the activity performed in the Gemba." [Page 6]

### Obeya
"obeya, a Japanese term used to designate the word 'big room'. This is a meeting room with visual resources which count on the presence of representatives of each discipline of the project with the objective that all have easy access to information, can make decisions, and solve operational problems." [Page 7]

### Lean Sustainability
"Lean sustainability is defined as 'the establishment of the long-term Lean implementation concept, the use of Lean for deep transformation, continuous optimization, and the effectuation of a Lean cultural atmosphere within the company.'" [Page 15]

### Digital Technologies Supporting Lean
"Lean approaches can be supported by digital technologies, such as the Internet of Things (IoT), Data Analytics, or autonomous robots." [Page 2] Tools cited that can be improved by digitalization: "Value Stream Mapping (VSM), the PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act) Cycle, and A3 thinking." [Page 2]

## 4. The Ten Critical Soft Skills (CSSs)

The paper identifies ten CSSs (codes from Table 1):

1. **(CUL)** "Implement a culture of waste elimination by simplifying project activities" — "the first CSS states the need to implement a culture of waste elimination in the company, simplifying all activities involved in the project." [Pages 5, 9]
2. **(AGI)** "Encourage the use of agile methodologies and quality management tools/concepts" — "The second CSS is based on fostering agile methodologies and quality management tools/concepts." [Pages 6, 9]
3. **(CHA)** "Ensure that necessary changes throughout the project are fully executed" — "The third CSS focuses on ensuring that the changes required throughout the project are fully executed." [Pages 6, 9]
4. **(GEM)** "Have the project team go to the Gemba and develop a vision of the whole" — "the fourth factor focuses on encouraging the project team to go to the Gemba and develop a broad view of everything that happens on and impacts a project, what we call the 'big picture view'." [Pages 6, 9]
5. **(CUS)** "Involve the customer in all phases of the project" — "the fifth CSS points to the need for the project team to involve the customer in all project phases." [Pages 6, 9]
6. **(KNO)** "Share the knowledge gained from each project" — "It is the sharing of knowledge obtained in each project, so those who succeed can have access to the positive and negative knowledge accumulated thus far." [Pages 6, 9]
7. **(COM)** "Ensure that communication flows well between all project stakeholders" — "It deals with the need to ensure that communication flows well among all project stakeholders." [Pages 6, 9]
8. **(INF)** "Make project information visually available, clear, and accessible" — "it is based on the visual availability of the primary information related to projects within the company." [Pages 7, 9]
9. **(OBE)** "Hold regular, agile meetings (obeyas) to manage smaller, achievable deliverables" — "the ninth CSS, which refers to holding regular agile meetings (known as obeyas) to manage smaller, achievable deliverables." [Pages 7, 9]
10. **(ENG)** "Stimulate Lean engagement among employees" — "the tenth CSS comes as a link that unites all of the other factors toward a common theme: the encouragement by management for all employees to be engaged in the Lean theme." [Pages 7, 9]

### Value within the Organization per CSS (Table 1, total citations per factor) [Page 9]

## 5. Methodology

### Research Steps / Literature Review
"The first step of the research encompasses a narrative review to identify the CSSs and was performed in the Scopus database." [Page 8] Keywords used: "'Project Management', 'Lean', 'Lean Manufacturing', 'Lean Project Management', 'digital transformation' and 'Industry 4.0'." [Page 8]

Inclusion and selection: "only articles published in Engineering, Business, management and accounting, Economics, Econometrics and Finance, Multidisciplinary areas were considered from a total of 2.835 articles to a bibliographic portfolio (BP) of 33 articles." [Page 8] The content analysis step states: "discussion and delimitation of the relevant material to be analyzed: 24 selected articles." [Page 8]

### Questionnaire
"a questionnaire was prepared with ten direct questions, each one referring to one of these CSSs. First, a pilot was applied to three experts." [Page 10] The survey was "divided into two main sections: information about the respondent, and the value of CSSs in implementing the LPM." [Page 10]

Likert scale: "1 = strongly disagree, 2 = disagree, 3 = indifferent, 4 = agree, 5 = strongly agree." [Page 10]

### Data Collection
"The web survey was sent from 1 March to 31 March 2021 to the most relevant and representative professional groups existing on LinkedIn." [Page 10] "199 experts agreed to participate. Of this total, 33 responses were excluded, totaling 166 valid responses representing a return of 83%." [Page 10] Sampling: "the sample is considered non-probabilistic (convenience sampling)." [Page 10]

### Data Analysis Techniques
- **Cronbach's alpha** used to estimate reliability; accepted range "the lower limit for the generally accepted Cronbach's alpha is 0.70, although it may decrease to 0.60 in exploratory research"; the study adopts "the value of α = 0.7 ... as the acceptable minimum, and α = 0.9 as the acceptable maximum." [Pages 10, 11]
- **Factor Analysis** (multivariate) "identifies strongly interrelated variables and proposes latent factors that summarize or explain the original set of variables with minimal loss of information." [Page 11]
- **KMO criterion** and **Bartlett's sphericity test** applied; "Factorability was examined by KMO test ... (KMO > 0.50) and Bartlett's sphericity test (significant at the 5% level)." [Pages 10, 11]
- **PCA** with "OBLIMIN rotation, considering eigenvalues greater than 1." [Page 11]

## 6. Results

### Respondent Profile (n = 166) [Page 11]
Role within organization: "Director 8%", "Manager 19%", "Leader 31%", "Project Team Member 42%", "Other 1%". Experience in Projects: "0–5 years 54%", "6–9 years 27%", "10–14 years 8%", "15–19 years 6%", "20 or more years 6%".

### Reliability
"Cronbach's alpha was calculated considering a value of 10 variables, finding a value of 0.828." [Page 11]

### Descriptive Findings
"Table 3 indicates that all respondents considered the CSSs surveyed by the literature exposed in this paper as relevant to the successful implementation of LPM in organizations." [Page 12] "all variables (except 9) did not obtain any 'strongly disagree' answers ... none of the variables achieved more than 7% in 'disagree' or 'indifferent' responses." [Page 12]

### Total Variance Explained (Table 5) [Page 14]
- Component 1: Initial eigenvalue total "4.168", Variance "41.680%", Cumulative "41.680%"
- Component 2: Initial eigenvalue total "0.973", Variance "9.733%", Cumulative "51.413%"
- Extraction Method: "Principal Component Analysis." [Page 14]

### Latent Factors Extracted
"it was possible to identify two latent factors emerging from the variables found in the literature review." [Page 12] "From the variables identified in the literature, 2 latent factors (marked in red) were extracted." [Page 13]

- **Dimension 1 (processes):** "Dimension 1 encompasses variables 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10, where a strong connection to implementing the LPM through processes was identified." [Page 15] Processes listed: "change management, knowledge management, communication management, visual management, people management, and quality management." [Page 15]
- **Dimension 2 (human relations):** "dimension 2, which encompasses variables 4 and 5, there is a dependence on human relations for the variables to be employed." [Page 15]

In the theoretical implications, these are named: "two latent factors, called 'implementation through processes' and 'implementation through people,' were identified in the principal component analysis." [Page 16]

### Rotated Component Matrix (Appendix B, Table A2) [Page 18]
Factor loadings — Component 1: V1 0.619, V2 0.820, V3 0.534, V6 0.730, V7 0.520, V8 0.690, V9 0.586, V10 0.777. Component 2: V4 0.910, V5 0.526. "Rotation Method: Oblimin with Kaiser Normalization." "Rotation converged in 6 iterations." [Page 18]

### Most Important CSSs by Dimension
Process view: "the most important CSSs are those related to the use of agile methodologies and the need to stimulate engagement in the adoption of Lean methodologies." [Page 16] People view: "the most important CSS is related to guaranteeing that the LPM teams go to the Gemba." [Page 16]

## 7. Framework

The framework presented in "Figure 4. Socio-technical Framework for Sustainable Value." [Page 16] The union of the two dimensions: "The union of dimensions 1 and 2 revealed after the factor analysis aims precisely to implement Lean in the long = term through people and processes, consolidating this methodology in the company and generating organizational value; therefore, Figure 4 below can be named Lean sustainability." [Page 15]

## 8. Conclusions and Implications

### Conclusion
"two latent factors were identified and structured in a socio-technical framework that aims to be a guideline for how to transform traditional project management into a Lean approach." [Page 16]

### Theoretical Implications
"a thorough study of the existing literature was conducted by identifying ten critical factors for implementing LPM. Through survey research, these factors were validated by professionals working in the project management field in Brazil." [Page 17]

### Practical Implications
"The proposed framework can help managers to guide decision making in LPM implementation by prioritizing the development of critical soft skills, which will help LPM to succeed ... and can also accelerate the transition to Industry 4.0." [Page 17]

### Limitations and Future Research
"future studies are expected to be conducted more comprehensively, comparing critical and latent factors of LPM implementation in different countries." [Page 17] "The main limitations of this article are the delimitation of the survey to professionals working in Brazilian companies and a sample size that was conditioned by the number of respondents of the questionnaire distributed electronically." [Page 17]
