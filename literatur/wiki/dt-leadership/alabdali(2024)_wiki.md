# Wiki: Alabdali et al. (2024) — Unveiling Green Digital Transformational Leadership

## 1. Title and Bibliographic Information

- **Title:** "Unveiling green digital transformational leadership: Nexus between green digital culture, green digital mindset, and green digital transformation" [Page 1]
- **Authors:** Mahmoud Abdulhadi Alabdali, Muhammad Zafar Yaqub, Reeti Agarwal (corresponding author), Hind Alofaysan, Amiya Kumar Mohapatra [Page 1]
- **Affiliations:** King Abdulaziz University, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia; Jaipuria Institute of Management, Lucknow, India; Princess Nourah bint Abdulrahman University, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia; Jaipuria Institute of Management, Indore, India [Page 1]
- **Journal:** "Journal of Cleaner Production 450 (2024) 141670" [Page 1]
- **Dates:** "Received 14 October 2023; Received in revised form 27 February 2024; Accepted 4 March 2024" [Page 1]
- **Keywords:** "Green digital transformational leadership; Green digital mindset; Green digital transformation; Organizational green digital culture; Sustainability; PLS-SEM" [Page 1]

## 2. Overview

The study's stated primary purpose is to "examine the association between Green Digital Transformational Leadership, Green Digital Mindset, and Green Digital Transformation Under Varying Permutations of Organizational Green Digital Culture." [Page 1]

It uses "the Stimuli-Organism-Response theory and the transformational leadership theory as the underpinning theoretical frameworks" and investigates "the direct, indirect, and moderated roles" of the constructs. [Page 1]

Method and headline result: "After performing PLS-based structural equation modeling on the data collected from 240 respondents approached via LinkedIn, a direct as well as indirect positive linkage between Green Digital Transformational Leadership, Green Digital Mindset, and Green Digital Transformation has been empirically substantiated." [Page 1]

The study also found a "significant positive moderation effect of Organizational Green Digital Culture on the Green Digital Transformational Leadership - Green Digital Mindset relationship." [Page 1]

### Research Questions [Page 2]
- **RQ1:** "Does GDTL indeed enhance employee GDM?"
- **RQ2:** "How can GDTLs impact the successful orchestration of GDT?"
- **RQ3:** "Does GDM mediate the impact of GDTL on GDT?"
- **RQ4:** "How does OGDC augment the development of GDM in employees through GDTL?"

### Stated Contributions [Page 2]
- "1) This research integrates and extends the transformational leadership theory (Bass and Avolio, 1993) with stimuli-organism-response model (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974) to the sustainably context to fathom the efficacy of GDTL in accentuating GDT through enhancing employee GDM under disrate contextual conditions."
- "2) This study makes vital theoretical and practical contributions by elucidating upon the mechanism and contextual conditions girdling employees' perceptions and receptivity to their GDT leaders' efforts."
- Use of "Middle Eastern data," providing "rare insights into the dynamics of green digital transformation in this unique Saudi Arabian context which is currently undergoing a social, digital, and ecological transformation under its overarching Vision 2030." [Page 2]

## 3. Core Concepts / Findings

### 3.1 Key Constructs (Definitions)

**Green Digital Transformational Leadership (GDTL) — the antecedent.** "GDTL is a leadership approach that intends to create a sustainable and environmentally conscious organizational culture." It "goes beyond standard forms of leadership... Instead, it places an emphasis on the significance of being a good steward of the environment." [Page 5] Earlier described as emphasizing "the role of leaders in not just prioritizing green initiatives but also enhance followers' technological readiness, spark creativity, and enable teamwork." This leadership style's "salient characteristics include sustainability, cooperation, ingenuity, and continuous improvement." [Page 2]

**Green Digital Transformation (GDT) — the outcome.** "Green Digital Transformation (GDT) refers to integrating digital technologies into an organization to enhance environmental sustainability by encouraging eco-friendly innovation, maximizing resource consumption, and reducing waste." [Page 5] Earlier: GDT "envisages digital technologies to facilitate ecological sustainability and advance sustainable practices within organizational settings," using "automation, data analytics, artificial intelligence, etc." [Page 2]

**Employees' Green Digital Mindset (EGDM / GDM).** "EGDM pertains to individuals' consciousness, comprehension, and motivation to utilize digital transformation ecologically sustainably." [Page 5] Earlier: GDM "pertains to employees' cognitive dispositions, convictions, and principles in the context of utilizing digital innovations to advance ecological sustainability." "Culminating EGDM may be an important precursor to GDT." [Page 2, Page 5]

**Organizational Green Digital Culture (OGDC) — the moderator.** "OGDC encompasses shared values, beliefs, and behaviors prioritizing sustainability through digital technologies. It fosters environmentally conscious practices using digital tools, encouraging employees' participation in sustainability initiatives." [Page 5]

**Control variables.** "this research used organization size (OS) and participants' age (age) as control variables." [Page 5]

### 3.2 Underpinning Theories

**Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) framework.** "The SOR framework suggests that external stimuli affect individuals, subsequently shaping their behaviors (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974)." In this study, "stimuli like GDTL style... and OGDC... may influence employees' (organism) perceptions and attitudes (including GDM), eventually shaping their behavioral responses... which would be their participation in GDT initiatives." [Page 4]

**Transformational leadership theory.** "TL theory (Bass and Avolio, 1993) signifies transformational leadership as a management concept that inspires employees to devise new approaches to expand and enhance an organization's success." Its critical facets "include idealized influence, inspirational motivation, individualized consideration, and intellectual stimulation (Bass, 1985)." "Integrating transformational leadership with green and digital leadership results in GDTL." [Page 4]

### 3.3 Hypotheses [Pages 5–6]

- **H1:** "GDTL has a direct positive association with (successful implementation of) GDT." [Page 5]
- **H2:** "GDTL has a significant positive association with employee GDM." [Page 6]
- **H3:** "Employees' GDM has a significant positive association with GDT." [Page 6]
- **H4:** "Employees' GDM mediates the relationship between GDTL and GDT." [Page 6]
- **H5:** "OGDC moderates the relationship between GDTL and employees' GDM." [Page 6]

### 3.4 Methodology

**Measurement scales.** "The first part contained the measurement scales for the four constructs adapted from previous studies." "GDTL was measured using six items adopted from (Chen and Chang, 2013). Six items to measure OGDC were adopted from Marshall et al. (2015). For GDM, seven items were adapted from Steg et al. (2005). Finally, the GDT was measured using a three-item scale adapted from Aral and Weill (2007)." Responses used "a seven-point Likert scale... with one point denoting 'strongly disagree' and seven points denoting 'strongly agree.'" The questionnaire was "translated into Arabic with subsequent back-translation to English, as recommended by Brislin (1980)." [Page 6]

**Pilot testing.** "The pilot study contained 40 samples for 'Cronbach's alpha (CA)' testing, and all elements' values met the threshold of more than 0.7." [Page 7]

**Sampling and data collection.** "The sampled population for this study is executives working in various firms in the Saudi Arabian industry." Minimum required sample size "was determined to be 137, given the medium effect size of 0.3 (Cohen, 1992), with a targeted statistical power level of 0.80, using four latent variables measured through 22 items at a significance level of 0.01." "Thus, the final sample size (n = 240) was larger than the suggested minimum of 137." Participants "were selected and approached via LinkedIn using the convenience and snowball sampling method." "Five hundred messages were sent to targeted participants via the LinkedIn message feature." The author's profile had "21 years of professional experience, with around 30,000 network members." [Page 7]

**Analytical strategy.** "The measurement and structural model have been analyzed using a PLS-based structural equation modeling (SEM) technique using SmartPLS 4.0 (Ringle et al., 2022)." Analyses "were performed in three steps": descriptive statistics, then the measurement model for "validity and reliability," and finally "testing the structural model." [Page 7]

### 3.5 Sample Profile [Page 7]
- "Women constituted 35% of the sample size, half of the participants were in their senior career level (51%), 70% of the participants were less than 40 years old, 95% of respondents were at a supervisor cadre or above, and 93% had a bachelor's degree or above." [Page 7]
- From Table 1: Saudi Arabia = 229 (96%); Male = 155 (65%), Female = 85 (35%); largest industry Healthcare = 50 (21%); largest function HR = 75 (31%); organization size >5000+ = 96 (40%). [Page 7]

### 3.6 Common Method Bias / Variance [Page 7]
"To identify problems with 'common method variance' (CMV) in PLS-SEM models, the MLMV methodology ('measured latent marker variable') was used (Chang et al., 2020)." "Table 2 shows that the R2 values of both GDM and GDT did not differ after adding the years of experience (YoE)... which suggested that the model of this study did not have CMB/CMV issues." A random variable was added and "the VIF was checked to determine if it was still less than 3.3 for both the inner and outer models... hence no CMV issue." [Page 7]

Table 2 values: GDM — Q2 = 0.187, R2 without marker = 0.228, R2 with marker = 0.237; GDT — Q2 = 0.128, R2 without marker = 0.217, R2 with marker = 0.228. [Page 8]

### 3.7 Measurement Model Assessment [Page 8]
- **Reliability / convergent validity:** "All factor loadings were significant and above 0.579." "All four constructs exhibited adequate reliability since the values of both C.A. and C.R. have been sufficiently above 0.7." "Convergent validity has been assessed through AVE, where all the values exceeded the threshold value of 0.50." [Page 8]
- **Discriminant validity:** ascertained "through Fornell Larcker Criterion (1981) and the heterotrait-monotrait ratio (HTMT) values"; "the square root of AVE in all instances has been greater than the correlations of the latent variables"; "all HTMT values have been less than 0.9." [Page 8]
- **Multicollinearity:** "the VIF is less than three for individual constructs; hence, multicollinearity is not an issue." [Page 8]
- Construct-level CA / CR / AVE (Table 3): GDM CA = 0.887, CR = 0.911, AVE = 0.593; GDT CA = 0.853, CR = 0.910, AVE = 0.772; GDTL CA = 0.882, CR = 0.911, AVE = 0.630; OGDC CA = 0.882, CR = 0.910, AVE = 0.629. [Page 8]

### 3.8 Structural Model — Fit, Explanatory and Predictive Power [Page 9]
- "The 'standardized root mean square residuals' (SRMR) are reported with a threshold value of less than 0.08." Table 5: SRMR saturated = 0.071, estimated = 0.079; NFI saturated = 0.816, estimated = 0.810. [Page 9, Page 10]
- "The R2 value for the endogenous construct was found to be 0.217. This value indicates that approximately 22% of the variance in the endogenous construct is explained by the variance of the exogenous constructs." This "suggests that the model has weak illustrative strength... which is still quite acceptable given the low number of predictors (only two)." [Page 9]
- "The f2 values are 0.038, 0.060, and 0.137, with the effect being between small to medium for H1, H2 and H3." [Page 9]
- "Q2 values were 0.187 and 0.128 for GDM and GDT, respectively, above 0, indicating that the model's predictive power was well established." [Page 9]

### 3.9 Hypothesis Test Results [Pages 9–10, Table 6]

All five hypotheses were "Supported." [Page 10]

| Hypothesis | Path | β | t | p | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | GDTL → GDT | 0.188 | 3.107 | 0.002 | Supported |
| H2 | GDTL → GDM | 0.276 | 3.612 | 0.000 | Supported |
| H3 | GDM → GDT | 0.358 | 5.560 | 0.000 | Supported |
| H4 | GDTL → GDM → GDT (Mediation) | 0.099 | 3.748 | 0.000 | Supported |
| H5 | OGDC × GDTL → GDM (Moderation) | 0.212 | 3.517 | 0.000 | Supported |

[Source: Table 6, Page 10]

- On H4: "the results support the mediation hypothesis, indicating that GDM partially mediates the positive influence of GDTL on GDT. Hence, GDTL not only directly influences GDT but also indirectly influences it through the development of GDM in employees." [Page 9]
- On H5: "the results support the hypothesis that the relationship between GDTL and GDM is moderated by GDC, indicating that the effect of GDTL on GDM varies depending on the level of GDC." [Page 9]

### 3.10 Control Variable Analysis [Pages 9–10, Table 7]
- "GDM significantly predicted GDT (β = 0.351, t = 5.299, p = 0.000)." [Page 9]
- Controlling for other variables, "GDTL predicted GDM (β = 0.291, t = 3.761, p = 0.000) and GDT (β = 0.206, t = 3.268, p = 0.001)." [Page 9]
- "OGC demonstrated a positive and statistically significant relationship with GDM (β = 0.231, t = 2.857, P = 0.004)." [Page 9]
- "the interaction between OGDC and GDTL also exhibited a statistically significant relationship with GDM (β = 0.193, t = 3.284, p = 0.001) after controlling for other variables." [Page 9]
- "the control variables OS and Age did not show statistically significant relationships with GDM or GDT." [Page 9]

### 3.11 Discussion Highlights [Pages 9–11]
- H1 finding "is in line with some of the previous studies, such as Farrukh et al. (2022) Hameed et al. (2022), Singh et al. (2020), Odugbesan et al. (2023)." [Page 9]
- H2 "attests to the efficacy of GDTL in nurturing employees' GDM." [Page 9]
- H5: "the impact of GDTL on GDM is more significant when organizational culture strongly endorses green initiatives and sustainability." [Page 11]
- Summary: "GDTLs' efforts to bring about a GDT could succeed if they could rally support from their followers through nurturing GDM within a workforce wherein the efficacy of GDTL to affect EGDM increases manifold if a potent OGDC is put in place." [Page 11]

### 3.12 Theoretical Contributions [Pages 11–12]
- Extends "the Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) framework and the transformational leadership theory to the GDT context." [Page 11]
- Enhances "understanding of the contextual conditions supporting GDT by examining the interaction between GDTL and OGDC in maturing EGDM." [Page 11]
- Provides "interdisciplinary insights and bridging the gap between many academic fields, such as corporate culture, environmental psychology, and leadership studies." [Page 11]
- "the newfangled model enhances our understanding of the (behavioral) dynamics of GDT in the unique but neglected Saudi (or Middle Eastern) context." [Page 12]

### 3.13 Practical Implications [Page 12]
- Organizations should "focus on GDTL development to enable digitalization-enabled eco-friendly initiatives and practices through training and development programs, mentoring, practical self-awareness, stakeholder engagement, etc." [Page 12]
- "an effective OGDC that fosters sustainability and digital innovation must be developed," e.g., "by creating green digital teams, deputing green digital champions." [Page 12]
- Organizations could "create effective configurations with GDTL, OGDC, and EGDM." [Page 12]

### 3.14 Conclusion, Limitations, Future Research [Pages 12–13]
- Conclusion: "The findings reveal a significant positive role of GDTL in affecting GDT directly and indirectly through maturing EGDM." [Page 12]
- **Limitation 1:** "the generalizability of the results may be constrained by the specific sample and context." Future studies should use "heterogeneous samples from various industries and cultural contexts." [Page 12]
- **Limitation 2:** "this research utilized a cross-sectional design"; "Longitudinal or experimental methodologies may offer a more comprehensive understanding of the causal associations." [Page 12]
- **Limitation 3:** "the research depended on self-report measures, which are susceptible to common method and potential response biases." [Page 13]
- **Limitation 4:** "the data utilized in this investigation were obtained from a single source." [Page 13]
- **Limitation 5:** future work "may focus on examining the influence of contextual conditions like digital maturity and digital capabilities." [Page 13]

### 3.16 Measurement Items (Appendix Table A1) [Pages 13–14]
- GDTL — 6 items, adopted from Chen and Chang (2013); e.g., "The leader provides a clear environmental and digital technology vision for the followers to follow." [Page 13]
- GDC — 6 items, adopted from Marshall et al. (2015); e.g., "The firm provides information to employees to understand the importance of sustainability and digitalization." [Page 13]
- GDM — 7 items, adopted from Steg et al. (2005); e.g., "I feel personally obligated to save as much energy as possible by using digital technology." [Page 13]
- GDT — 3 items, adopted from Aral and Weill (2007); e.g., "Our firm is driving new business processes built on [green] technologies." [Page 13]
