# Müller, Konzag, Nielsen & Sandholt (2024) — DT Leadership Contingency Framework

**Journal:** International Journal of Information Management 75 (2024) 102734  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijinfomgt.2023.102734  
**Citations:** 76  
**Type:** Systematic literature review + theoretical framework (67 papers, Scopus + WoS, 2011–2021)

---

## Summary

Müller et al. argue that there is no single universal set of DT leadership competencies — instead, the required competencies depend on what is *driving* the transformation and what *goals* it pursues. Drawing on Cameron & Quinn's (2006) Competing Values Framework (CVF), they theorize four **archetypical competency portfolios** (the challenger, bricoleur, organizer, competitor), each suited to different organizational contexts. This is the first contingency-based treatment of DT leadership competencies in the literature.

---

## Core Framework: Four Competency Portfolios

Built on two CVF dimensions:
- **Flexibility ↔ Stability** (organizational orientation)
- **Internal ↔ External** focus (transformation goals)

| Portfolio          | Quadrant               | Goal                            | Key competency categories                                                                   |
| ------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Challenger** | External + Flexibility | Explore market innovation       | Technology scouting; Stimulating alternate business futures; Entrepreneurial spirit         |
| **The Bricoleur**  | Internal + Stability   | Support operational efficiency  | Technology-culture fit; Process-technology alignment; Stakeholder coordination              |
| **The Organizer**  | Internal + Flexibility | Ensure stakeholder involvement  | Digital communication tools; Team collaboration; Trust in digital technologies              |
| **The Competitor** | External + Stability   | Improve competitive positioning | Competitive positioning analysis; Cultivating competitive capabilities; Product co-creation |

All four portfolios require **technical, business, and people-oriented** competencies — just in different combinations.

---

## Key Claims

- **No one-size-fits-all.** Digital transformation is a multifaceted phenomenon; a single competency list cannot capture what leaders need under varying circumstances.
- **Leaders don't need deep technical knowledge.** Consensus across 67 papers: a general understanding of digital tools is sufficient; deep tech expertise sits elsewhere in the org.
- **People-oriented competencies are universally needed** across all four portfolios — the inner/relational dimension is non-negotiable regardless of context.
- **Competency needs evolve** as organizations move through DT maturity levels (bricoleur → challenger trajectory as maturity increases).
- Directly critiques [[source-schiuma-2022-transformative-leadership-compass]]: Schiuma identifies *what* competencies are needed but not *when* and *why* different ones apply.

---

## Five Propositions

1. Leaders need combinations of technical, business, and people-oriented competencies depending on DT drivers/goals.
2. Flexibility + external focus → **Challenger** is the preferred leader type.
3. Stability + internal focus → **Bricoleur** is preferred.
4. Flexibility + internal focus → **Organizer** is preferred.
5. Stability + external focus → **Competitor** is preferred.

---

## Limitations (stated)

- No original empirical data — based entirely on existing literature.
- Covers only competencies needed *during* transformation (not before/after).
- Does not address how organizational context (size, sector, public vs. private) shapes needs.
- Does not fully explain how leaders develop or acquire these competency portfolios.

## Key Insight for Your Artifact

The contingency finding is actually an argument *for* the IDG approach: if people-oriented (inner) competencies appear in **all four** portfolios regardless of context, then IDG — as a structured taxonomy of those inner capacities — is the most universally applicable foundation. Schiuma tells you *what* to be good at; Müller shows it matters *everywhere*; IDG tells you *how to develop it*.
