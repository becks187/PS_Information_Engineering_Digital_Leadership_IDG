# Measuring the Human Dimensions of Digital Leadership for Successful Digital Transformation

## Overview

This paper, published in *Research-Technology Management* (Vol. 65 No. 3, May–June 2022, pp. 39–49), presents a Digital Leadership Scale with 15 human dimensions for successful digital transformation leadership. The authors conducted 13 semi-structured interviews with successful leaders of digitally mature organizations across various industries. The study identifies 15 key human dimensions organized according to Stephen Covey's 4 Cores of Credibility: Integrity, Intent, Capabilities, and Results. The paper aims to "capture the qualities of leaders leading digitally mature organizations so that leaders of digitally developing organizations can increase their likelihood of success."

**Authors:** Haroon Abbu (Bell & Howell), Paul Mugge (NC State University), Gerhard Gudergan, Gerrit Hoeborn, Alexander Kwiatkowski (FIR Institute at RWTH Aachen University)

**Keywords:** Digital leadership, Trust, Leadership assessment, Digital transformation

## Core Concepts / Findings

### 1. Study Motivation

- "Digital transformation is more about people than it is about digital technology." [Page 2, Lines 39–40]
- "It requires organizational changes that are backed by leadership and driven by radical challenges to corporate culture." [Page 2, Lines 40–42]
- Organizations fall into two categories: "digitally developing and digitally mature" [Page 2, Lines 43–44]
- "Digitally mature organizations are managed differently than digitally developing organizations. Their leaders align human and financial resources with the company's strategy; create a collaborative, and nimble development environment; and promote open and transparent communication" [Page 2, Lines 45–50]
- "Currently, no established practical method exists to assess the human attributes of successful digital leaders." [Page 3, Lines 101–103]

### 2. Methodology

- "We interviewed 13 successful leaders of digitally mature organizations." [Page 3, Lines 108–110]
- Interviewees: "seven CEOs, two business unit heads (BUHs) developing digital products, two functional heads (FHs) leading corporate functions, and two chief digital officers (CDOs)" [Page 3, Lines 156–158]
- "Together, the digital leaders we interviewed represented companies with combined revenues of more than $225B and nearly 1.2 million employees." [Page 3, Lines 160–161]
- The study used "maximum variation strategy of purposeful sampling" across "geographic representation, size of the company, gender, and industry representation" [Page 3, Lines 119–123]
- Between August and October 2020, "13 semi-structured interviews (3 women, 10 men)" were conducted [Page 3, Lines 131–132]
- "We used an inductive approach to reduce the extensive text data gained from the interviews" [Page 3, Lines 162–163]
- "We based our interpretations solely on what the interviewees said." [Page 3, Lines 170–171]

### 3. Participant Quotes

- A managing director: "The most successful leaders that I've seen have an innate ability to connect the dots—to be systems thinkers—and can communicate extremely effectively to their team and to their clients. A digital leader thus also needs to be a good storyteller." [Page 3–4, Lines 190–193]
- A CEO of a mechanical engineering business: "Be genuinely interested in your employees. I expect absolute transparency and openness. I encourage employees to speak their minds and share their opinions." [Page 4, Lines 213–217]
- A CTO: "As we consider putting the AI to work, we have a responsibility to ensure fairness and to make sure that we can explain how the model arrives at a recommendation." [Page 4, Lines 218–221]
- An energy company CEO: "Trust has created a much greater willingness to provide relevant data and information to each other." [Page 4, Lines 222–225]
- An automotive strategy director: "You need role models and people who have actual experience in digital. In the end, it is essential for digital leaders not just to talk about digitalization, but also to prove they are able to deliver results." [Page 4, Lines 227–230]

### 4. The 15 Dimensions of the Digital Leadership Scale

The dimensions are categorized according to "Stephen Covey's (2006) 4 Cores of Credibility: Integrity, Intent, Capabilities, and Results" [Page 4, Lines 244–246]:

1. **Honesty** (Integrity): "An assessment of the leader's moral character that connotes positive and virtuous attributes such as integrity, truthfulness, and straightforwardness. Honesty also involves being trustworthy, loyal, fair, and sincere." Mentioned by 7 participants. [Page 4, Lines 248, 264–267]
2. **Humility** (Integrity): "An assessment of the leader's modest view of their own importance—that is, their humbleness." Mentioned by 6 participants. [Page 5, Lines 304–307, 322–324]
3. **Courage** (Integrity): "An assessment of the leader's mental or moral strength to venture, persevere, and withstand danger, fear, or difficulty." Mentioned by 7 participants. [Page 5–6, Lines 333–337, 358–360]
4. **Ethical AI** (Integrity): "An assessment of a leader's ability to do everything in their power to ensure the results of AI and other technologies employing machine learning models, are fair and explainable." Mentioned by 4 participants. [Page 6, Lines 369, 386–389]
5. **Growth Mindset** (Intent): "An assessment of the leader's desire to tackle new challenges and grow as a person." Mentioned by 7 participants. [Page 6, Lines 400–402, 421–422]
6. **Transparent Agenda** (Intent): "An assessment of a leader's ability to openly share information about the business strategy with employees, which is necessary for collaboration, cooperation, and collective decision-making." Mentioned by 7 participants. [Page 6–7, Lines 432, 456–459]
7. **Data Focus** (Intent): "An assessment of a leader's ability to encourage the application of data and analytics everywhere, including their own decision-making." Mentioned by 7 participants. [Page 7, Lines 468, 482–484]
8. **Inspire Engagement** (Intent): "An assessment of a leader's ability to stimulate people's interest in or enthusiasm for doing something." Mentioned by 7 participants. [Page 7, Lines 492, 512–514]
9. **Storytelling** (Intent): "An assessment of the leader to inspire employees and gain buy-in." Mentioned by 5 participants. [Page 7–8, Lines 522, 541–543]
10. **Digital Literacy** (Capabilities): "An assessment of a leader's ability to develop digital competencies within the leadership team." Mentioned by 7 participants. [Page 8, Lines 559, 574–576]
11. **Positive Attitude** (Capabilities): "An assessment of a leader's ability to carry themselves like champions and purposefully show their positivity in how they behave and communicate." Mentioned by 5 participants. [Page 8, Lines 587, 599–601]
12. **Skills Acquisition** (Capabilities): "An assessment of a leader's ability to influence digital talent acquisition and development for the organization." Mentioned by 8 participants. [Page 8, Lines 608, 623–625]
13. **Knowledge Sharing** (Capabilities): "An assessment of a leader's ability to enable access to knowledge and improve learning." Mentioned by 9 participants. [Page 8–9, Lines 634, 646–648]
14. **Participative Style** (Capabilities): "An assessment of a leader's ability to develop a collaborative and inclusive way to approach problems and opportunities." Mentioned by 8 participants. [Page 9, Lines 661, 685–687]
15. **Track Record** (Results): "An assessment of the leader's digital initiatives to produce meaningful results." Mentioned by 13 participants (most mentioned). [Page 9, Lines 695, 707–708]

### 5. Key Managerial Takeaways

- "Building trust and credibility are at the core of effective digital leadership. Credible leaders walk the talk, and employees learn by observing their leaders." [Page 9, Lines 720–722]
- "Employees should be at the center of your digital strategy. Align employees with business goals and clearly communicate your plan and vision for the company to them." [Page 9, Lines 723–727]
- "Digital leaders must make conscious efforts to ensure trustworthiness in the use of digital technologies such as AI and machine learning. They must ensure these technologies are used in a way that's fair, respectful of individual privacy, and beneficial to society." [Page 9, Lines 730–734]

### 6. Future Research Plans

- "We are developing a Digital Leadership Scale to measure these 15 dimensions on a five-point Likert scale." [Page 9, Lines 741–743]
- "Based on the scale, we plan to create a user-friendly self-assessment tool and make it available for free to anyone leading a digital initiative—at the project, function, business unit, or company level." [Page 9–10, Lines 747–750]

### 7. Limitations

- "The dimensions presented should be checked for their distinctiveness. Within the research discussions, for example, it emerged that the Storytelling dimension can be a sub-item of the Inspire Engagement dimension." [Page 10, Lines 764–767]
- "Measuring character traits is admittedly difficult, especially since the measurements are mostly self-reported questionnaires." [Page 10, Lines 768–769]
- "Additionally, the inductive reasoning approach used is limited as it begins with our observation of the role of digital leadership in digital transformation success." [Page 10, Lines 774–776]

### 8. Conclusion

- "We presented a prescriptive assessment for leaders in digital transformation by identifying the human dimensions required to successfully lead a digital transformation initiative." [Page 10, Lines 784–786]
- "The overarching insight derived from this study is that the human dimensions of leadership are critical to a successful digital transformation." [Page 10, Lines 791–793]
