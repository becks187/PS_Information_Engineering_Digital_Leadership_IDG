# Wiki: The Role of Leadership in Managing Digital Transformation — A Systematic Literature Review (Rakovic et al., 2024)

## 1. Title and Bibliographic Information

- **Title:** "The role of leadership in managing digital transformation: A systematic literature review" [Page 1]
- **Authors:** Lazar Rakovic, Slobodan Maric (corresponding author), Lena Djordjevic Milutinovic, Vuk Vukovic, Radmila Bjekic [Page 1]
- **Affiliations:** University of Novi Sad, Faculty of Economics in Subotica (Departments for Business Informatics and for Management) and University of Belgrade, Faculty of Organisational Sciences [Page 1]
- **Publication:** "E&M Economics and Management, 27(2), 87–107." [Page 2] DOI: 10.15240/tul/001/2024-2-006 [Page 1]
- **JEL Classification:** "M10, M15." [Page 1]
- **Keywords:** "Digital transformation, leadership, digital leadership, systematic literature review." [Page 1]

## 2. Overview

The paper is a systematic literature review addressing the role of leadership in managing digital transformation.

- **Research problem:** "The authors of this article identify a vaguely described process of digital transformation as the main research problem, highlighting the need for the systematisation of leadership competencies necessary for effective digital transformation management." [Page 1]
- **Objectives:** "the research objectives are to define and describe the process of digital transformation and identify the leadership competencies necessary for successful management of the digital transformation process." [Page 1]
- **Method and scope:** "a systematic literature review was conducted for the period 2001–2022 and 90 papers were identified based on the criteria outlined by the Web of Science and Scopus index databases." [Page 1]
- **Analytical lens:** "The identified papers were then analysed from the aspect of defining digital transformation, the activities of the digital transformation process, and the characteristics of digital leadership." [Page 1]
- **Main contribution:** "the paper provides a comprehensive definition of digital transformation, proposes a framework for its management, and analyses the key competencies of digital transformation leaders (digital leaders), which together make up the paper's main contribution." [Page 1]
- **Stated limitations:** "Only two, albeit the largest, citation databases were searched, and only papers in English were identified. The established framework should be verified and expanded through empirical research." [Page 1]

Core premise: "Digital transformation should not be seen as a time-bound project, but rather as a process that needs to be continuously improved and managed over time. In the absence of leadership, digital transformation will occur spontaneously in an organisation, but it is necessary to monitor and manage the process itself to achieve the goal and establish a digital organisation." [Page 1]

## 3. Core Concepts / Findings

### 3.1 Motivation and Background (Introduction)

- The field is fragmented: "The existing literature within the research field is insufficiently homogeneous and inadequately integrated, resulting in insufficient knowledge about the competencies required for leaders to successfully carry out the process of digital transformation (Müller et al., 2024)." [Page 2]
- Leadership and digital transformation are inseparable: "Leadership and digital transformation cannot be considered separately, but they must support each other in the application and sustainability of any digital transformation initiative in an organisation (Jayawardena et al., 2020; Zulu & Khosrowshahi, 2021)." [Page 2]
- Digital transformation is "the driver of positive but also devastating market changes (Al-Nuaimi et al., 2022; Kokolek et al., 2019) and represents more than just a technological evolution (Criado-Perez et al., 2022)." [Page 2]
- "A key finding of their research is that the classic all-encompassing leadership approach cannot guide digital transformation." [Page 2] (referring to Müller et al., 2024)
- Four archetypical competency portfolios are described: "the challenger (exploring the market innovations), the bricoleur (supporting operational efficiency), the organiser (ensuring active stakeholder participation), and the competitor (improving competitive position; Müller et al., 2024)." [Page 2 / Page 3]
- Five strategic actions identified by Kringelum et al. (2024): "i) discussion and communication about vision and strategy; ii) aligning resources and activities with strategy; iii) developing the competencies of top managers, middle managers and employees; iv) ensuring operational improvements and efficiency; and v) creating an orientation toward customer or user needs and expectations." [Page 3]
- "Digital transformation includes digitisation and digitalisation (Paul et al., 2024), but digital transformation is a more complex and comprehensive concept (O'Leary, 2023)." [Page 3]
- Failure statistic: "However, 70% of digital transformation initiatives fail to achieve their objectives (Alshehab et al., 2022)." [Page 3]
- Research problem and main objective: "a research problem was identified in the form of a vaguely described and defined digital transformation process, along with the need to systematise the leadership competencies required to successfully manage digital transformation." [Page 3] "The main objective of the research is to define and describe the process of digital transformation as well as to identify the necessary leadership competencies for the successful management of the digital transformation process." [Page 3 / Page 4]

### 3.2 Research Methodology

The methodology follows three basic segments (Xiao & Watson, 2019): "i) planning the review; ii) conducting the review; and iii) reporting the review (Fig. 1)." [Page 4]

The process consists of nine steps [Page 4]:
1. Define a specific research problem (general research questions). [Page 4]
2. Develop a complete research algorithm (protocol). [Page 4]
3. Initial search within selected databases by title, abstract and key words. [Page 4]
4. Screening for inclusion by analysing abstracts according to the inclusion criteria. [Page 4]
5. Duplicate elimination (two databases: WoS and Scopus, period 2001–2022). [Page 4]
6. Evaluating the quality of the complete papers. [Page 4]
7. Additional review through forward and backward research. [Page 4]
8. Analysis and synthesis of the papers. [Page 4]
9. Review report creation (final step). [Page 4]

**Research questions** [Page 4]:
- "RQ1: What is implied by the term digital transformation and what are the challenges, factors and steps of the digital transformation process?" [Page 4]
- "RQ2: What leadership competencies and characteristics are necessary to successfully lead the digital transformation process?" [Page 4]

**Search details:**
- "The search was performed for the period from 2001 to 2022." [Page 5]
- "Based on the research question, the keywords related to 'leadership' and 'digital transformation' were selected." [Page 5]; keyword string in Fig. 1: "Leadership* AND "Digital transformation"*" [Page 5]
- Three search channels: "i) the mentioned electronic databases (WoS and Scopus); ii) backward searching ...; and iii) forward searching..." [Page 5]
- Initial hits (identical for both author groups): "WoS n = 439 and Scopus n = 328." [Page 6]
- Two author groups A and B conducted the review independently with alignment/consultation. [Page 6]

**Three inclusion criteria** (a paper must meet at least one) [Page 6]:
1. "defining digital transformation" [Page 6]
2. "the conceptual framework, or steps of digital transformation" [Page 6]
3. "the leadership characteristics necessary for the successful management of the digital transformation process." [Page 6]

**Screening / counts:**
- Group A: WoS n = 85 (excluding 354); Scopus n = 123 (excluding 205). [Page 6]
- Group B: WoS n = 101 (excluding 338); Scopus n = 91 (excluding 237). [Page 6]
- After merging groups and removing duplicates: WoS = 67 papers; Scopus = 104 papers. [Page 6]
- After combining both databases and removing duplicates: "129 papers remained, while 42 papers were excluded." [Page 6]
- Quality assessment: "79 papers were selected and 50 were excluded (preliminary final list, n = 79, excluding n = 50)." [Page 7]
- Forward/backward search added papers: "an additional 19 papers were included on that basis (for and back search, n = 11), bringing the total number of papers selected to be 90." [Page 7] (Note: text states "an additional 19 papers" but the parenthetical and Fig. 1 indicate "n = 11" / "Including: (n=11)") [Page 5, Page 7]
- "Final number of papers (n=90)." [Page 5 / Page 7]

**Data extraction and synthesis:**
- Two extraction topics: "i) definition of digital transformation and conceptual framework of digital transformation; and ii) characteristics, role and style of leadership." [Page 7]
- "Data extraction from the selected papers is guided by the method of synthesis selected, which, in this case, is a textual narrative synthesis." [Page 7]
- "The method of contingent design was used as the main method of analysis and synthesis of the extracted data, which represents a cyclical research synthesis." [Page 7]

### 3.3 Defining Digital Transformation

- "The authors generally agree that digital transformation is a process (Egor, 2020; Engesmo & Panteli, 2021; Lorentzen, 2022) that manifests itself in different ways and with different intensity (Engesmo & Panteli, 2021)." [Page 7]
- "It can be said that digital transformation is an inevitable and irreversible process (Zhu et al., 2021)..." [Page 7]
- "Many organisations focus digital transformation too much on technology alone. This is precisely why many digital transformation processes fail (Jayawardena et al., 2020)." [Page 7]
- Frequently cited technologies: "social media, internet of things, mobility, big data and analytics, cloud computing, artificial intelligence and blockchain (Florek-Paszkowska et al., 2021; Tekic & Koroteev, 2019)." [Page 7]
- Carcary et al. (2016) key areas: "promoting and implementing an agile digital culture, developing digital leadership skills, building digital talent and defining and implementing a transformative digital business strategy." [Pages 7–8]
- "According to Zhu et al. (2021), digital transformation implies a transition from a product-centric to a customer-centric strategy." [Page 8]
- Florek-Paszkowska et al. (2021) group levers into "human-based and non-human based factors." [Page 8]
- Guinan et al. (2019) team-based levers: "diverse team composition, iterative goal setting, continuous learning and talent management." [Pages 8–9]
- Reis and Melao (2023) three elements: "the technological digital transformation," "the structural digital transformation," and "the human digital transformation." [Page 9]
- "According to Weritz et al. (2020) three main parts of digital transformation are products (services), clients and processes." [Page 9]

**Table 1 — Definitions of digital transformation [Page 8]** (selected sources):
- Reis and Melao (2023): "Digital transformation is the use of new digital technologies that enables major business improvements and influences all aspects of customers' lives." [Page 8]
- Vial (2019): "Digital transformation is a process that aims to improve an entity by triggering significant changes to its properties through combinations of information, computing, communication, and connectivity technologies." [Page 8]
- Other sources tabulated: Kutnjak et al. (2019), Brown and Brown (2019), Venkatesh et al. (2019), Tu and Chi (2020), Fachrunnisa et al. (2020), Abdallah et al. (2021). [Page 8]

### 3.4 The Framework of Digital Transformation

- "The road map for digital transformation should be developed, which will guide the organisation to where it wants to be in the future (Evans et al., 2021)." [Page 9]
- Kokolek et al. (2019) six key factors prerequisite for success: "digital knowledge and skills, ability to apply new technologies to the business, continuous learning, strategic vision, network leadership and agility." [Page 9]
- "Imran et al. (2021) identify leadership, organisational structure, and culture as the main drivers of digital transformation." [Page 9]

**Table 2 — Challenges of digital transformation [Page 9]:**
- "Limited infrastructure" — Hai et al. (2021) [Page 9]
- "Lack of skills" — Hai et al. (2021) [Page 9]
- "Not adapted management, lack of knowledge to lead digital transformation" — Hai et al. (2021), Salume et al. (2021) [Page 9]
- "Lack of resources to incorporate digital technologies" — Van Tonder et al. (2020) [Page 9]
- "Culture in an organisation" — Evans et al. (2021) [Page 9]
- "Employee resistance" — Trenerry et al. (2021) [Page 9]

**Table 3 — Key factors of digital transformation [Page 10]:** Customer centricity; Corporate processes and structure; Digital knowledge and skills; Agility; Leadership; Continuous learning/upgrading/retraining; Commitment of the management; Strategic vision, creation of the strategic framework of DT; Ability to apply new technologies to the business; Possessing the necessary capabilities to ensure digital transformation; Creating a digital transformation team; Culture and people. [Page 10]

**Table 4 — Steps/dimensions of digital transformation [Page 11]** (compiled across Van Tonder et al. (2020), Gaffley and Pelser (2021), Evans et al. (2021), Jacobi and Brenner (2018), Peter et al. (2020), Andriole (2020)): includes "Install digital leadership," "Development of new products/services and/or digitalisation of existing ones," "Development of employees' digital competences," "Digitalisation of customer platforms (channels)," "Transforming the user experience," "Identifying and acquiring the necessary resources," "Digitalisation of business processes," "Changing/modifying the organisation – structure, incentives and culture," "Digitalisation of existing capabilities," "Building a digital strategy," "Creating cross-functional teams," "Building stronger ties with stakeholders," "Collecting digital knowledge and experience at the organisational level," "Creation and measurement of key performance areas KPAs." [Page 11]
- Note on steps: "It is important to consider that they are not sequential and should be implemented according to the needs of the industry and organisation on the path to digital transformation." [Page 10]

### 3.5 Leadership in the Process of Digital Transformation

- Three aspects regarding the definition of digital leadership were found. "Firstly, existing literature does not clearly define the relationship between e-leadership and digital leadership." [Page 10] "Secondly, the analysed literature provides a concise definition of digital leadership that blurs the distinction between e-leadership and digital leadership." [Page 11] "Thirdly, the results show that e-leadership and digital leadership are not synonyms, suggesting that digital leadership is much broader than e-leadership..." [Page 11]
- "Digital leadership entails the ability to direct resources and utilise information communication technologies to achieve the organisation's goals, significantly contributing to organisational performance (Suryadi Muslim & Setyono, 2024)." [Page 11]
- "Digital leadership is a combination of a transformational leadership style and the use of digital technology (Mihardjo & Rukmana, 2019), or even a combination of digital competencies and digital culture (Mihardjo et al., 2019)." [Pages 11–12]
- "It is also important to highlight that ineffective leadership is the primary cause of digital transformation failure (Aditya et al., 2021)." [Page 13]
- Three leadership styles and four platforms (Tanniru et al., 2018): "administrative leadership, adaptive leadership, and enabling leadership... and four platforms – an adoption platform, a learning platform, an innovation platform, and a system and business platform." [Page 13]
- "One of the key competencies of a leader in the digital era is trust (Savolainen, 2018), which can be the basis of a separate leadership style." [Page 13]
- "However, a conclusion can be found that there is no single conceptual framework of digital leadership competencies, which are not unified (Wahyuningsih & Asri, 2024)." [Page 13]

**Table 5 — Competencies/skills/characteristics of digital leaders [Page 12]** (selected sources):
- Karakose et al. (2021): "Use of technology, managerial skills, individual skills" [Page 12]
- Zivkovic (2022): "Vision, innovation, flexibility, understanding of digital technologies, collaboration, empowerment, multiple intelligences, continuous learning, experimentation" [Page 12]
- Guzmán et al. (2020): "Cognitive skills, interpersonal skills, strategic skills" [Page 12]
- Cortellazzo et al. (2019): "Digital skills, human skills, change initiation, problem solving" [Page 12]
- Schiuma et al. (2022): "Mentor, pragmatist, catalyst, communicator, righteous, facilitator" [Page 12]
- McCarthy et al. (2021): "Digital architect, digital strategist, digital culturalist, customer centrist, business process optimiser, digital workplace landscaper, data advocate, organisational agilist" [Page 12]
- Additional sources tabulated: Gilli et al. (2023), Imran et al. (2021), Schiuma et al. (2021), Abdallah et al. (2021), Bygstad et al. (2017), Cuya and Bayona-Oré (2022), Eberl and Drews (2021), Larjovuori et al. (2018), Mihardjo et al. (2019), Noonpakdee et al. (2020). [Page 12]

### 3.6 Conclusions and Discussion

- "Generally, the authors agree that digital transformation is not a one-time project, but rather a process that manifests itself in different ways and at different intensities." [Pages 13–14]
- "Without management, digital transformation will happen spontaneously in the organisation, but to achieve the desired results, it must be monitored and managed." [Page 14]
- "The literature does not offer a generic and generally accepted definition of digital strategy." [Page 14]
- Characteristics attributed to digital transformation include: "digital transformation is a continuous process of changing business processes; digital transformation includes the creation of new or the modification of existing business models; digital transformation is more than the implementation of new technologies..." [Page 14]
- **Framework of digital transformation (Fig. 2):** "Identifying the challenges of digital transformation" → "Initiating actions to overcome the challenges of digital transformation" → "developing a digital strategy, installing digital leadership, and creating and measuring key performance areas (Fig. 2)." [Pages 14–15]
- "All of the above supports the position that leadership (people, strategy, leadership, business processes and technology) is the fundamental component of digital transformation (Abbu et al., 2022; Abdallah et al., 2021; Grigore & Coman, 2018)." [Page 15]
- "A chief executive officer (CEO) plays a very important role in the transformation process..." [Page 15]

### 3.7 Contribution, Limitations and Future Research

- Contribution: "Both practitioners and researchers can benefit from a clearer definition of digital transformation. This proposed framework can serve as a starting point for digital transformation research... In establishing training requirements and recruiting adequate employees, HR management can utilise the proposed competencies list." [Page 15]
- Limitations: "Only two, albeit the largest, citation databases were searched, and only papers in English were identified. The established framework should be verified and expanded through empirical research (Gudergan et al., 2021)..." [Page 15]
- Future research: "Future research should focus on the relationship between digital leadership and organisational performance (Abbu et al., 2022), as well as the development of the digital maturity assessment model..." [Page 15]
