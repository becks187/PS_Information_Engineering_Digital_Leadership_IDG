# Wiki: Butt (2020) — A Conceptual Framework to Support Digital Transformation in Manufacturing Using an Integrated Business Process Management Approach

## 1. Overview

**Title (verbatim):** "A Conceptual Framework to Support Digital Transformation in Manufacturing Using an Integrated Business Process Management Approach" [Page 1].

**Author:** "Javaid Butt", "School of Engineering and Built Environment, Anglia Ruskin University, Chelmsford CM1 1SQ, Essex, UK" [Page 1].

**Publication:** "Designs 2020, 4, 17; doi:10.3390/designs4030017" [Page 1]; "Received: 31 May 2020; Accepted: 23 June 2020; Published: 27 June 2020" [Page 1].

**Stated purpose:** "this paper presents a conceptual framework based on business process management (BPM). The framework is informed by a content-centric literature review of Industry 4.0 technologies, its design principles, and BPM method." [Page 1]. Further: "This paper aims to oﬀer an integrative business process management framework that can be used as a reference model by academics and manufacturing organizations, in their journey towards a successful transition from traditional manufacturing to Industry 4.0." [Page 3].

**Keywords (verbatim):** "business process management; Industry 4.0; design principles; lean six sigma; change management; skills gap analysis; digital transformation" [Page 1].

**Identified problems with Industry 4.0 adoption (verbatim):** "a lack of standardized implementation protocols, emphasis on the introduction of new technologies without assessing their role within the business, the compartmentalization of digital initiatives from the rest of the business, and the large-scale implementation of digitalization without a realistic view of return on investment." [Page 1].

**Central research question (verbatim):** "how can BPM be used to support digital transformation through Industry 4.0?" [Page 11].

**Methodology (verbatim):** "this study adopted a narrative literature review strategy [152], to propose a conceptual integrated business process management (IBPM) framework rooted in the traditional BPM method to support digital transformation." [Page 11]. Also: "This work is not focused on developing a systematic literature review, but presenting a conceptual framework supported by literature" [Page 12].

**Paper structure (verbatim):** "Section 2 presents a review of Industry 4.0 key technology trends and Section 3 presents an overview of its design principles. Section 4 is focused on business process management whereas Section 5 presents the conceptualization and complete breakdown of the proposed integrated business process management framework." [Page 3].

## 2. Key Technology Trends of Industry 4.0 (Section 2)

### 2.1 Additive Manufacturing (AM)
"This is an umbrella term that encompasses a group of technologies capable of manufacturing three-dimensional objects by adding layers on top of each other, using diﬀerent materials based on a computer-aided design ﬁle of a product" [Page 3]. "AM is a key player when it comes to product customization, design ﬂexibility, time and cost-eﬀectiveness, and reducing the total number of parts through part consolidation" [Page 3].

### 2.2 Augmented Reality (AR)
"AR augments 3D graphics in a real-world environment, whereas VR is an artiﬁcial environment that is created with software and presented to the user in a way that the user accepts it as a real environment" [Page 3]. "AR is beneﬁtting the manufacturing sector by providing better insights for product development, equipment maintenance, workforce training, production ﬂoor issues, process ﬂow, and quality assurance" [Page 4].

### 2.3 Simulation and Modelling Techniques
"They serve an extremely useful purpose by providing a visual representation of a product or process, and showing the eﬀects of diﬀerent conditions in a virtual environment." [Page 3]. Product/process simulation methods: "the ﬁnite element method, ﬁnite volume method, and discrete element method"; packages "include CAEplex, MATLAB, Ansys, OpenFOAM, and EMS." [Page 4]. Process-flow paradigms: "process-centric, systems dynamics, Petri net, Monte Carlo simulations, and agent-based"; packages "include, but are not limited to, WITNESS, AnyLogic, FlexSim, Simio, and Simul8 Professional." [Page 4].

### 2.4 Autonomous Robots
"This has also given rise to 'cobots', or collaborative robots that work closely with humans and augment their capabilities." [Page 4]. Per the International Federation of Robotics there are "four types of collaborative manufacturing applications, i.e., co-existence ..., sequential collaboration ..., co-operation ..., and responsive collaboration (robot responds to the human movements in real-time)." [Page 4].

### 2.5 Internet of Things (IoT / IIoT)
"Internet of Things (IoT) is a system of interconnected devices implanted with sensors, software, and electronics to initiate the exchange and collation of data" [Page 5]. "IIoT systems are conceived as a layered modular architecture of digital technology with four layers, i.e., device, network, service, and content." [Page 5]. "5G enabled IIoT systems will accelerate the realization of Industry 4.0, with high-speed, low-latency, and large-volume data transfers" [Page 5].

### 2.6 Big Data Analytics (BDA)
"'Big data' refers to a collection of massive data sets that cannot be analyzed through conventional means. There are four types of data, i.e., structured, semi-structured, quasi-structured, and unstructured." [Page 5]. Four types of analytics: "The ﬁrst is descriptive ...The second is diagnostic ...Predictive is the third ...The last is prescriptive, that recommends actions and strategies based on advanced analytical tools" [Page 5]. Tools: "Xplenty, Skytree, Talend, Lumify" [Page 5].

### 2.7 Cloud Computing (CC)
NIST definition (verbatim): "a model for enabling convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of conﬁgurable computing resources (e.g., networks, servers, storage, applications, and services) that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management eﬀort or cloud provider interaction" [Page 5]. "CC operates in four diﬀerent types, i.e., public cloud ..., private cloud ..., hybrid cloud ..., and community cloud (shared by several organizations)" [Pages 5–6].

### 2.8 Cybersecurity (CS)
"The ﬂow of enormous data sets in and out of an organization over the Internet makes cybersecurity (CS) a key element of Industry 4.0." [Page 6]. Named regulations/standards: "the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) from the European Union and the UK Data Protection Act 2018" and "The most common ones are ISO/IEC 27001 and 27002, part of the ISO/IEC 27000 family of standards" [Page 6].

### 2.9 Horizontal and Vertical Integration
"Horizontal integration is about digitalization across the full value and supply chain, focusing on data exchanges and connected information systems." [Page 6]. "vertical integration creates a comprehensive solution by integrating IT systems at various hierarchical production and manufacturing levels." The hierarchical levels listed: "the ﬁeld level ..., the control level ..., the production process level ..., the operations level ... and the enterprise planning level (order management and processing)." [Page 6].

### 2.10 Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS)
"Cyber-physical systems (CPS) are systems of collaborating computational entities that are in intensive connection with the surrounding physical world and its on-going processes ..." [Page 6]. "CPS is usually based on a 5C architecture of connection, conversion, cyber, cognition, and conﬁguration [88]. However, it has been extended to include customer, content, and coalition" [Page 6]. "The use of CPS in manufacturing is referred to as CPPS (cyber-physical production systems)" [Page 7].

### 2.11 Cyber Manufacturing (CM)
"Cyber manufacturing (CM) is a concept derived from CPS and refers to a system that oﬀers an information-transparent environment, to facilitate asset management, provide reconﬁgurability, and maintain productivity." [Page 7]. "The enabling technologies of CM include IoT, BDA, and CPS." [Page 7].

## 3. Industry 4.0 Design Principles (Section 3)

### 3.1 Modularity
"It refers to the design of system components and is characterized by the capability of system components to be removed or added in an eﬃcient manner [104], leading to agile manufacturing systems rather than linear ones" [Page 7]. "System components are loosely coupled and can be reconﬁgured on a plug-and-play principle" [Page 7].

### 3.2 Interoperability
Defined (verbatim) as "the exchange of data among systems through the use of Industry 4.0 enabling technologies [2]." [Page 7]. "Interoperability in Industry 4.0 can be deﬁned in four diﬀerent levels, i.e., operational, semantic, systematical, and technical interoperability" [Page 7].

### 3.3 Decentralization
"allows independent operations as well as autonomous decisions through various components in a system rather than a centralized system, enabling ﬂexibility and adaptability" [Page 7]. "CPS and digital twins are among the key enablers of decentralization" [Page 7].

### 3.4 Virtualization
"This design principle refers to two diﬀerent scenarios. First is the case where one virtual resource is created from multiple physical resources, and in the second scenario, many virtual resources are created from one or more physical resources." [Page 8]. "Digital twins can serve a variety of purposes, e.g., performance optimization, visualizing diﬀerent scenarios, and real-time control." [Page 8].

### 3.5 Real-Time Capability
"One of the main features of Industry 4.0 is the agility of operations that cannot happen without real-time data collation and analysis supporting decision making, and even real-time cyber-security attack detection" [Page 8]. "Real-time capability supports the other design principles and can be considered as an essential design principle when moving towards digital transformation." [Page 8].

### 3.6 Service Orientation
"Service orientation allows manufacturing organizations to sell both their products and services to customers." [Page 9]. "shifting the focus from products to customers and from mass production to tailored services." [Page 9].

## 4. Business Process Management (Section 4)

**Business process (verbatim):** "A business process is a set of related functions (and sub-functions) in a speciﬁc sequence performed by people or machinery that can deliver value to an internal or external customer." [Page 9].

**Two classifications of business processes:**
- Rosing, Von Scheel, and Scheer: "main/core processes (focus on delivering value), management processes (focus on overseeing main processes), and supporting processes (focus on supporting the main processes)" [Page 9].
- Kirchmer: "operational processes (primary business processes that create value), management processes ..., and governance processes (ensure compliance with legal guidelines)." [Page 9].

**BPR (Business Process Reengineering):** "BPR was introduced in the early 1990s and aimed to help organizations fundamentally rethink the workﬂow of their business processes and incorporate radical changes ..." [Page 9].

**BPM (verbatim):** "BPM is a management discipline that provides governance for a process-oriented organization through business process modelling, execution, and evaluation, with the goal of agility and operational performance" [Page 10].

**Six core elements of BPM (verbatim):** "there are six core elements that are critical to the success of BPM initiatives, and these are strategic alignment, governance, methods, information technology, people, and culture" [Page 10].

**Six Sigma in BPM:** "Six sigma is a popular and useful approach for BPM comprising DMAIC (deﬁne, measure, analyze, improve, control) and DFSS (Design for Six Sigma) approaches" [Page 10].

**BPM Everywhere (BPME):** "a new direction known as BPM Everywhere (BPME) shows promise as a way of blending traditional process techniques, with additional capabilities to automate the handling of all the independent devices." [Page 10].

## 5. The Integrated Business Process Management (IBPM) Framework (Section 5)

**Origin of the overlooked factors (verbatim):** "These factors have been identiﬁed by the author through his work on a European funded project, Growing into Industry 4.0 (focused on identifying the barriers related to the uptake of Industry 4.0 [18])" [Page 12].

**Literature support categories (Table 1, verbatim main topics):** "BPM"; "Skills gap"; "Risk management"; "Change management"; "Cost-beneﬁt analysis"; "Lean/Six Sigma/Lean Six Sigma" [Page 12].

### Phase 1: Process Identification
"a business problem is posed by the IBPM team, comprised of senior management members, process owners, process participants, process analysts, and system engineers from each department of the organization." [Page 12]. Supporting tools: "a SWOT (strength-weakness-opportunity-threat) analysis" [Page 13]; plus "organizational charts ..., stakeholder maps ..., context diagram ..., and business use case diagrams" [Page 13].

### Phase 2: Process Discovery
"The second phase involves the identiﬁcation of the business process to be improved or reengineered." [Page 14]. "it must be drawn as 'it-is' (or 'as-is') to ensure that the shortcomings could be captured" [Page 14]. Tools include "LucidChart, Microsoft Visio, and Creately"; these work on "business process modelling notation (BPMN)" [Page 14]; also "Value stream mapping" [Page 14].

### Phase 3: Process Analysis
"The idea is to deﬁne key performance indicators (KPIs), metrics, key risk indicators (KRIs), and process performance indicators (PPIs)." [Page 14]. Definitions: "A KPI is a measure of performance and success [211], whereas a metric is a quantiﬁable number within a KPI that helps track performance and progress" [Page 14]; "KRI is an indicator of the possibility of future adverse impact" [Page 14]; "PPIs are deﬁned as quantiﬁable metrics that allow the evaluation of the eﬃciency and eﬀectiveness of business processes." [Page 15]. Indicators "should satisfy the SMART (speciﬁc-measurable-assignable-realistic-time related) criteria" [Page 15]. Three types of process interdependence: "pooled, sequential, and reciprocal" [Page 15]. Qualitative technique: "value-added analysis (VAA)" distinguishing "business value-added (BVA), value-added (VA), or non-value-added (NVA) activities" [Page 16].

### Phase 4: Process Redesign or Reengineering
"The goal of this phase is to identify changes to the process that would help to address the issues identiﬁed in the previous phase." [Page 16]. "The output of this phase is typically a 'to-be' process model" [Page 16]. Named tools: "FMEA (failure mode and eﬀects analysis) or a cause-and-eﬀect diagram" [Page 16]; "TRIZ (theory of inventive problem-solving)" [Page 16]; "the Pugh Matrix" [Page 16]. The reengineering example leads to "the development of CPS (Section 2.10) in manufacturing called cyber manufacturing (Section 2.11)." [Page 17].

### Phase 5: Streamlining Business Processes
"it is vital to streamline other business processes, to ensure that they can still work cohesively without causing disruption." [Page 19]. Example: "enhancing the manufacturing capacity will automatically increase sales ...This will put a strain on the sales department" [Page 19].

### Phase 6: Risk Management and Contingency Planning
"The IBPM team should incorporate methods of avoiding, mitigating, transferring, and accepting risks." [Page 20]. Risk identification methods: "brainstorming ..., premortem ..., or counterfactual thinking" [Page 20]. Distinction: "Risk management includes steps before a risk occurs, whereas contingency planning focuses on steps to be taken if, or when, a risk occurs" [Page 20].

### Phase 7: Skills Gap Analysis
"the IBPM team should conduct a skills gap analysis, to analyze the existing skills of the workforce and the required skills, based on the strategic goals of the organization." [Page 21]. "Work 4.0 ...is an umbrella term that encompasses and discusses the challenges of changing work due to Industry 4.0 enabling technologies" [Page 21]. "Worker 4.0 is such a framework that highlights the hybrid competencies (a mixture of adaptive, technological, and technical skills) that the future workforce should possess" [Page 21]. "the concept of Operator 4.0, that represents the 'operator of the future'" — examples: "an analytical operator who uses BDA [241], an augmented operator who uses AR [167], and a smarter operator who uses AI-based technologies" [Page 21].

### Phase 8: Change Management
"Change management is a collective term for all approaches to prepare, support, and help individuals, teams, and organizations in making organizational changes" [Page 22]. Named models: "Lewin's change management model, McKinsey 7S model, Kotter's change management theory, and ADKAR model." [Page 23]. Lewin's model: "three steps of unfreeze, change, and refreeze." [Page 23]. "ADKAR is an acronym for awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, and reinforcement" [Page 23]. "Adoption management is about creating lasting change through culture, education, and technology that can lead to continuous change" [Page 23].

### Phase 9: Cost-Benefit Analysis
"the IBPM team needs to consider every single beneﬁt that can result by employing Industry 4.0 enabling technologies, convert the beneﬁts into monetary value, and judge against the associated costs." [Page 24]. Metrics: "return on investment (ROI), payback period (PP), and beneﬁts to cost ratio (BCR)." [Page 23]. Named framework: "Phillips ROI Methodology™, comprising 10 steps divided into four phases [260]" — phases "evaluation planning ..., data collection ..., data analysis ..., [and] the reporting phase" [Page 24]. Formulas shown in Table 3: "ROI = XX−YY / YY ∗100 = ZZ%"; "PP = YY / XX ∗12 = ZZ months"; "BCR = YY / XX = ZZ:1" [Page 25].

### Phase 10: Process Validation and Implementation
"This phase focuses on validating the simulation models developed in Phase 4 to an acceptable level of diﬀerence." [Page 25]. "several scaled pilot runs could be conducted" [Page 25]; "The team should ensure that the pilot is reversible and does not signiﬁcantly disrupt the normal business processes." [Page 25]. "some members from the original IBPM team should be trained to become 'digital champions'" [Page 26].

### Phase 11: Process Monitoring and Controlling
"relevant data should be collated by the IBPM team to perform a post-implementation review of the new process for continuous improvements." [Page 26]. "Lean six sigma approaches would be a good ﬁt. In this case, DMAIC (deﬁne, measure, analyze, improve, control) would work well" [Page 26].

## 6. Conclusions (Section 6)

**How IBPM differs from traditional BPM (verbatim):** "Firstly, it incorporates factors that are often overlooked in Industry 4.0 implementation, e.g., skills gap analysis, risk management, contingency planning, change management, and cost-beneﬁt analysis. ...Secondly, it does not show over-reliance on technological tools for the implementation, but provides a more human-centric approach" [Page 27].

**Positioning of IBPM (verbatim):** "IBPM should be considered as an evolved version of BPM, as a response to the rapid technological changes that have signiﬁcantly transformed the manufacturing sector." [Page 27].

**Stated limitation / future work (verbatim):** "It is to be noted that there are no standardized protocols for Industry 4.0 implementation, and any implementation strategy, such as IBPM proposed in this work, should be adapted based on the nature of the business" [Page 27]. "The next stage of the work would be the implementation of the IBPM framework in a manufacturing organization for digital transformation." [Page 27].

**Funding / Conflicts (verbatim):** "Funding: This research did not receive any external funding." and "Conﬂicts of Interest: There is only one author; hence, there is no conﬂict of interest." [Page 27].
