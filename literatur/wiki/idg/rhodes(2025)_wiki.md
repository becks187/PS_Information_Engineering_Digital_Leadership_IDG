# Measuring and Training Inner Development Goals

**Authors:** Jonathan Rhodes, Jaime Blakeley-Glover, Andy Miller, Alan Taylor
**Venue:** Book chapter (Chapter 9), DOI: 10.1515/9783111453729-010
**Method:** Scale development (focus groups + pilot study) + quasi-experimental intervention

---

## Research Objective

Develop a validated instrument to measure IDGs (the IDG-A scale) and a training intervention (the MAP model) to develop inner capacities for climate action within organizations.

## The Problem

- 93% of companies struggle to inspire employees to drive climate initiatives (Accenture, 2022)
- 98% of climate programs fail; 81% deemed only "moderate value" (Bain, Davis-Peccoud 2017)
- Root cause: disconnect between organizational sustainability goals and individual employees' intrinsic motivation — knowledge exists but action falters
- Existing coaching frameworks (GROW, solution-focused) work for individuals but fail at systemic/team change

## The IDG-A Scale (Inner Development Goals — Adapted)

### Development Process
1. **CEO focus group** (6 CEOs): validated 5 IDG dimensions but identified a missing **6th dimension: "organizational belonging"** — "the IDG framework misses the link between the individual and their working environment, which is where the change is set to occur"
2. **Executive coaching focus group** (5 coaches): independently arrived at a similar addition, calling it **"culture connection"** — aligning personal values to organization, role clarity, feeling heard
3. **Student group**: refined questions into 29-item Likert questionnaire (items 1-23 map to original IDG skills; items 24-29 capture the new organizational belonging dimension)

### The 29-Item IDG-A Questionnaire (Table 9.1)

Items map to 6 dimensions:
- **Being** (items 1-5): values/purpose awareness, sincerity/integrity, vulnerability/openness, reflectiveness, presence
- **Thinking** (items 6-9): critical review without bias, systemic/complex thinking, perspective-taking, sensemaking
- **Relating** (items 10-14): long-term vision/commitment, appreciation/gratitude, connectedness, selfless action, empathy/compassion
- **Collaborating** (items 15-19): genuine dialogue/conflict management, collaborative relationships/psychological safety, diversity/inclusion, trust, mobilization
- **Acting** (items 20-23): courage/decisive action, creativity/innovation, optimism/hope, perseverance/patience
- **Organizational Belonging** (items 24-29): values-organization alignment, role clarity/autonomy, collective stress culture, contribution/voice in meetings, strategy dissemination/belonging, collective vision/progress tracking

### Validation
- **Pilot study:** 39 participants from 4 global organizations, selected for sustainability involvement
- **Reliability:** IDG-A reliability = **0.89** (strong; >0.8 threshold)
- **Inter-domain correlations:** contrary to expectation, domains showed noteworthy relationships — total score is a robust indicator of overall inner development; domains are not mutually exclusive but form a cohesive framework

## The MAP Model (Meaning, Awareness, Purpose)

A coaching process designed to bridge the gap between intention and action for climate/sustainability goals.

### Structure
The MAP model progresses through two cycles (Figure 9.1):
1. **Individual cycle:** Meaning → Awareness → Purpose → Action
2. **Team cycle:** Meaning → Awareness → Purpose → Action

After one complete individual+team cycle, the numbers and direction become self-sustaining ("moot").

### Key Design Principles
- Start with **"Why should you personally care?"** not organizational goals — intrinsic motivation first
- Use **positive imagery** and mental time-travel (imagine successful future 25 years out) to anchor motivation
- Personalize thought processes — align emotions, values, mindsets, attitudes, cognitions, and behaviors with the issue
- Progress from individual meaning → personal action → team meaning → team action

### Experimental Results
- **Two organizations**, similar baseline IDG-A scores
- **Control group:** carbon literacy training (15 hours over 10 weeks) — conventional top-down knowledge approach
- **MAP group:** 9 hours over 10 weeks — employee-driven, inner development approach
- **Result:** IDG-A scores soared in MAP group while carbon literacy group remained static — significant difference between groups
- Carbon literacy participants reported valuable education but experienced it as "obligatory exercise divorced from their inner world"
- MAP participants reported deeper personal breakthroughs and self-initiated climate action projects

## Key Insight: The 6th Dimension

The discovery of **organizational belonging** as a 6th IDG dimension is significant:
- Both CEO and coaching focus groups independently identified it
- It bridges individual inner development and organizational context
- Items include: values-org alignment, role clarity, feeling heard, strategy transparency, collective stress support
- This maps directly to the proseminar's concern with organizational/team context in DT leadership

## Relevance to the Proseminar Paper

- **IDG-A scale** provides an operationalized, validated measurement instrument for IDG skills — important for arguing that IDG is not just conceptual but empirically measurable
- **6th dimension (organizational belonging)** strengthens the argument that IDG needs contextual adaptation — the proseminar's artifact for DT leaders is another such adaptation
- **MAP model** shows IDG skills can be trained through coaching — relevant if the proseminar artifact includes developmental recommendations
- **Experimental evidence** that inner development outperforms knowledge-only training for behavioral change — supports the overall argument that inner skills matter more than information
- The reliability of 0.89 for the IDG-A scale gives quantitative credibility to IDG as a framework
