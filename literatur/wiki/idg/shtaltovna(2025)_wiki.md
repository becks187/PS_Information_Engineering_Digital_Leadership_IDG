# Transdisciplinary Competencies for the Future

**Authors:** Yuliya Shtaltovna (Hochschule Fresenius, Berlin / Kyiv School of Economics) & Rusudan Makhachashvili (Borys Grinchenko Kyiv Metropolitan University)
**Venue:** WMSCI 2025 (29th World Multi-Conference on Systemics, Cybernetics and Informatics)
**DOI:** 10.54808/WMSCI2025.01.341

---

## Research Objective

Examine the gap between transdisciplinary future skills highlighted in WEF "Future of Jobs" reports and those sought by learners in Coursera's Global Skills Index. Use the [[concept-idg-framework]] as a bridging taxonomy to propose a strategy roadmap for Humanity-focused Higher Education (HiEd).

## Method

- Systematic literature review + comparative content analysis benchmarking WEF Future of Jobs reports (2016–2023) and Coursera Global Skills Reports (2018–2023)
- Modelling of interoperability between competency frameworks and digital/AI literacy requirements across 6 frameworks: EU E-Competence, UNESCO AI Competence, DigComp 2.2, UNESCO AI & DT Competency, WEF Skills in the Age of AI, Europe's Digital Decade
- Survey of 372 respondents (Arts & Sciences educators/students in Ukraine, 2022–2025) — pandemic and wartime emergency digitization context
- Mixed methods: 16 diagnostic questions (multiple choice, criteria comparison, Likert scale)

## Key Findings

### 1. IDG-to-WEF Skills Mapping (Figure 1)

The paper explicitly maps core Global Skills onto all 5 IDG dimensions:

| IDG Dimension | Mapped WEF/Global Skills |
|---------------|--------------------------|
| **Being** | Personal resilience, EI, active learning; inner peace, self-awareness via stress tolerance |
| **Thinking** | Critical thinking, complexity awareness, analytical thinking, complex problem-solving; perspective skills, sense-making |
| **Relating** | EI, empathy, compassion, connectedness; leadership through understanding and influencing others |
| **Collaborating** | Teamwork, social interaction, communication skills, inclusive mindset; leading diverse teams |
| **Acting** | Creativity, courage, agency, perseverance; proactive engagement and innovation; continuous learning and adaptation |

### 2. Skills Heat Map — Coursera 2018–2023 (Table 1)

| Trend | Skills |
|-------|--------|
| **Consistent** | Leadership & Management, Communication, Business Strategy, Entrepreneurship, Project Management |
| **Rising** | Digital Marketing, Sustainability Management, Innovation |
| **Emerging** | Diversity & Inclusion |
| **Declining** | Accounting, Human Resources, Sales |

Key pattern: socio-emotional skills ("Attitudes" in WEF taxonomy) are reaching parity with technical skills; leadership and social influence surpassing core worker skills as strategic priorities (2023–2027).

### 3. Interoperability of Soft Skills Across Digital Frameworks (Figure 3)

Communication and collaboration appear across virtually all 6 digital competence frameworks. The authors define an **interoperable digital skill** as a "rhizomatic (virally acquired) capability of mutually informed manipulation of digital data, tools, and communication formats."

Four IDG-interoperable core skills in digital education (Figure 4):

| | Individual Focus | Group Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Advanced** | Cognitive Creativity & Flexibility | Leadership & Social Influence |
| **Basic** | Attention to Detail | Collaboration |

### 4. AI-Proof vs. AI-Ready Skills

- **AI-proof skills:** empathy, compassion, complex problem-solving, ethical judgment — inherently human, hard for AI to replicate; emphasize social dynamics and moral reasoning
- **AI-ready skills:** sense-making, creativity, innovation, learning mindset — enable effective collaboration with AI technologies
- IDG cultivates both categories simultaneously

### 5. Survey Results (n=372)

- Dominant professional e-competence dimensions: PLAN (61%), RUN, ENABLE (with AI for communication/education)
- ICT and Gen AI user skills: 75% of respondents; practitioner skills: 74%
- Top student-oriented goals: help learn independently via AI tools (66%), become effective digital citizens (61%), solve problems with AI (59%), think critically with AI (54%)
- Dominant AI-interoperable skills acquired: communication, emotional intellect, creativity, problem-solving, innovation

### 6. Transdisciplinary Skill Domains

Six key domains of human activity where skills integrate across frameworks:
1. Communication
2. Cognitive Activity
3. Personal Interaction
4. Social Activity
5. Heuristics
6. AI-Interaction

## Conceptual Framework

The paper introduces an **AI-interoperable Communication Cycle** (Figure 2) grounded in Shannon's communication model, where communication is the core factor of interoperability between source and target knowledge domains — for both human and machine interaction.

## Recommendations

- **For individuals:** embrace IDG, embed EQ, adaptability, self-awareness into curricular and extracurricular activities; modules on self-reflection, mindfulness, collaborative learning
- **For organizations (HEIs):** adopt IDG as core faculty development component; training on empathy, creativity, critical thinking
- **For policymakers:** align SMART university curricula with IDG principles; prioritize holistic, learner-centered development; project-based learning with ethical considerations

## Limitations

- Sample is Arts & Sciences educators in Ukraine — not generalizable to technical/IT leadership contexts
- Wartime emergency context is unique
- Survey is self-reported Likert-scale; no causal claims

## Relevance to the Proseminar Paper
