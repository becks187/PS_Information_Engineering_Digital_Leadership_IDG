# Positioning and Presenting DSR for Maximum Impact

**Authors:** Shirley Gregor, Alan R. Hevner
**Venue:** MIS Quarterly, Vol. 37, No. 2, pp. 337–355 (June 2013)
**Method:** DSR approach (meta-level: the paper itself uses DSRM to develop its frameworks)

---

## Research Objective

Help researchers (1) appreciate levels of artifact abstraction as DSR contributions, (2) identify appropriate ways of consuming and producing knowledge, (3) understand and position knowledge contributions, and (4) structure a DSR article for maximum impact.

## Two Types of Knowledge in DSR

| Type | Symbol | Definition | Examples |
|------|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Descriptive** | Omega (Omega) | The "what" — knowledge about natural/artificial/human phenomena | Observations, classifications, laws, regularities, principles, patterns |
| **Prescriptive** | Lambda (Lambda) | The "how" — knowledge of human-built artifacts | Constructs, models, methods, instantiations, design theory |

Both types comprise the DSR knowledge base (Figure 1). DSR draws from Omega knowledge and contributes to Lambda knowledge — and vice versa via evaluation.

## Three Levels of Artifact Abstraction (Table 1)

| Level | Contribution Type | Example Artifacts | Knowledge Maturity |
|-------|------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Level 3** | Well-developed design theory about embedded phenomena | Design theories (mid-range and grand theories) | More abstract, complete, mature |
| **Level 2** | Nascent design theory — knowledge as operational principles/architecture | Constructs, methods, models, design principles, technological rules | ↕ |
| **Level 1** | Situated implementation of artifact | Software products, implemented processes | More specific, limited, less mature |

Key insight: All three levels can be valid DSR contributions. An artifact at Level 1 (instantiation) can be a contribution even without abstraction to design theory, because *demonstrating that something can be built* is itself knowledge.

## The Knowledge Contribution Framework (Figure 3)

A 2x2 matrix based on **problem maturity** (application domain maturity) and **solution maturity**:

| | High Application Domain Maturity | Low Application Domain Maturity |
|---|---|---|
| **Low Solution Maturity** | **Improvement:** New solutions for known problems. Research opportunity + knowledge contribution | **Invention:** New solutions for new problems. Research opportunity + knowledge contribution |
| **High Solution Maturity** | **Routine Design:** Known solutions for known problems. No major knowledge contribution | **Exaptation:** Extend known solutions to new problems (adopt from other fields). Research opportunity + knowledge contribution |

### The Four Quadrants

- **Invention** — radical breakthrough; rare; no prior art for problem or solution. Example: first DSS (Scott-Morton 1967)
- **Improvement** — most common in IS DSR; better solution for known problem. Must demonstrate clear advance over existing solutions. 10 of 13 MISQ DSR papers (2006–2011) were improvements
- **Exaptation** — extend existing artifact from one field to a new problem domain. 3 of 13 papers. Requires showing the new field has challenges not present in original domain
- **Routine Design** — no research contribution; professional practice applying known solutions to known problems

## DSR Publication Schema (Table 3)

A 7-section template for structuring a DSR paper:

| Section | Contents |
|---------|----------|
| **1. Introduction** | Problem definition, research objectives, goals for the artifact, scope, significance |
| **2. Literature Review** | Prior design theory/knowledge (Lambda), descriptive theory (Omega), existing artifacts, justificatory knowledge (kernel theory) |
| **3. Method** | Specific DSR approach adopted (cite [[source-hevner-2004-dsr-guidelines]], [[source-peffers-2007-dsrm]]); rationale for build/evaluate choices |
| **4. Artifact Description** | Concise description at appropriate abstraction level; design search process; the major part of the paper |
| **5. Evaluation** | Evidence of utility — validity, quality, efficacy via analytics, case studies, experiments, simulations |
| **6. Discussion** | What was learned, comparison with prior work, limitations, theoretical and practical significance |
| **7. Conclusions** | Restate important findings; "declaration of victory" |

## Kernel Theory / Justificatory Knowledge

- **Kernel theory** (Walls et al. 1992): theories from natural/social sciences that inform artifact construction
- The paper uses the broader term **justificatory knowledge** — any descriptive knowledge that informs why the design works
- A mature body of design knowledge should include kernel theory explaining *why* the design works

## Highlighting the Knowledge Contribution

Three questions reviewers ask (adapted from Wilson 2002):
1. **Is it new?** ("What's new?")
2. **Is it interesting?** ("So what?")
3. **Is it true/well-done?** ("Why should I believe you?")

The contribution type (invention/improvement/exaptation) should be explicitly stated. The introduction must position the paper against problem and solution maturity.

## Relevance to the Proseminar Paper

- **Positioning the artifact:** The proseminar paper is an **exaptation** — it takes the IDG framework (existing solution from sustainability leadership) and extends it to a new problem domain (DT leadership competencies). This is the clearest way to frame the knowledge contribution
- **Artifact abstraction level:** The proseminar artifact (competency framework mapping) is a **Level 2** contribution — nascent design theory as operational principles/model, not yet a full design theory (Level 3) nor a situated implementation (Level 1)
- **Publication schema:** Table 3 provides the structural template for the proseminar paper — adapt the 7-section structure to the proseminar format
- **Justificatory knowledge:** The DT leadership literature ([[synthesis-theme1-dt-leadership-frameworks]]) and inner skills literature ([[synthesis-theme2-inner-skills-bridge]]) serve as the Omega knowledge base; IDG and GLfS serve as the Lambda knowledge base
