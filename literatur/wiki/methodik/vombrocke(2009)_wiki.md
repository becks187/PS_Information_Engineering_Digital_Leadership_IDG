# Reconstructing the Giant: On the Importance of Rigour in Documenting the Literature Search Process

**Authors:** Jan vom Brocke, Alexander Simons, Bjorn Niehaves, Kai Riemer, Ralf Plattfaut, Anne Cleven
**Venue:** ECIS 2009 Conference Paper (June 2009)
**Citations:** 618+
**Method:** Meta-review of 25 review articles from 10 top-ranked IS journals

---

## Research Objective

Investigate whether IS researchers comprehensibly document the process of literature search in their review articles, and propose guidelines for more rigorous search documentation.

## The Problem

- Literature reviews are central to scholarship, yet the term "review" is ambiguous (Garfield 1987)
- The quality of a review is largely determined by the quality of the literature search process
- Metaphor: a literature search "reconstructs the giant" (Newton's shoulders) — the search is the skeleton that determines the reconstruction
- 647+ active IS journals make exhaustive search impossible — therefore transparent documentation of inclusion/exclusion is critical
- RQ: *Do IS researchers comprehensibly document the process of literature search in their review articles?*

## Study Design

- Analyzed review articles from the 10 top-ranked IS journals (per AIS consolidated list)
- Excluded HBR (#8, not peer-reviewed) and IEEE Transactions (#9, too heterogeneous)
- Searched using the key phrase "literature review" across databases (Figure 1)
- Evaluated each review article against: number of articles, period covered, journals/databases explored, keywords used, backward/forward search conducted

## Key Findings (Figure 2)

- **6 of 25 reviews (~25%)** provide no information at all on the underlying search process
- Only **15** document how many articles were included; only **11** state precisely *which* articles
- Only **~50%** explicate the time period covered
- Only **2 articles** comprehensibly document search phrases (Gerwin & Barrowman 2002; Ngai & Gunasekaran 2007)
- Many reviewers searched by *journal* rather than by *topic* — contradicting [[source-webster-watson-2002-lit-review]] guidance
- Backward/forward search was rarely documented

## Three Propositions for Why

1. **P1:** IS researchers cannot refer to established guidelines for documenting the literature search process
2. **P2:** IS researchers are not fully aware of the importance of rigorously documenting the literature search
3. **P3:** The conditions of the publication process (page limits, reviewer demands) often prevent detailed search documentation

## 5-Phase Literature Review Framework (Figure 3)

A cyclical framework reflecting the iterative nature of reviewing:

| Phase | Activity |
|-------|----------|
| **I. Definition of review scope** | Define scope and flavour using Cooper's taxonomy |
| **II. Conceptualization of topic** | Broad conception of what is known and where knowledge gaps exist; working definitions |
| **III. Literature search** | Journal search, database search, keyword search, backward search, forward search (Figure 5) |
| **IV. Literature analysis and synthesis** | Analyze and synthesize using concept matrix ([[source-webster-watson-2002-lit-review]]) |
| **V. Research agenda** | Derive sharper questions for future research from gaps in the concept matrix |

## Cooper's Taxonomy of Literature Reviews (Figure 4)

Six characteristics to define review scope (Cooper 1988):

| Characteristic | Categories |
|----------------|------------|
| **(1) Focus** | Research outcomes, research methods, theories, applications |
| **(2) Goal** | Integration, criticism, central issues |
| **(3) Organization** | Historical, conceptual, methodological |
| **(4) Perspective** | Neutral representation, espousal of position |
| **(5) Audience** | Specialized scholars, general scholars, practitioners/politicians, general public |
| **(6) Coverage** | Exhaustive, exhaustive and selective, representative, central/pivotal |

## Literature Search Process (Figure 5)

Detailed sub-phases for Phase III:

1. **Journal search** — identify leading journals in the field
2. **Database search** — identify databases providing access to those journals
3. **Keyword search** — query databases with precise search phrases; document keywords
4. **Backward search** — review references of identified articles
5. **Forward search** — use citation indices (e.g., ISI Web of Knowledge) to find citing articles
6. **Literature evaluation** — title/abstract/full text screening at every stage

## Plea for Rigour

- A well-documented search can be extended, transferred, and replicated
- Documentation should include: databases, keywords, time period, number of hits, inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Journals should establish dedicated review sections and not impose page limits that force cutting search documentation
- The search process documentation should be a review criterion for judging literature review quality

## Relevance to the Proseminar Paper

- **Cooper's taxonomy:** Use to explicitly define the scope of the proseminar's literature review (focus: theories/applications; goal: integration; coverage: selective; audience: specialized scholars)
- **5-phase framework:** Provides the procedural backbone for the literature review section — cite alongside [[source-webster-watson-2002-lit-review]]
- **Search documentation:** The proseminar should transparently document databases searched, keywords used, and inclusion/exclusion criteria — even briefly
- **Complementary to Webster & Watson:** vom Brocke adds the *search documentation rigour* dimension that Webster & Watson's concept-centric approach assumes but does not operationalize
