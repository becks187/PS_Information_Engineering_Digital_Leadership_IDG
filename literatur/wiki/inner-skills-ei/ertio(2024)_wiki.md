# Ertiö, Eriksson, Rowan & McCarthy (2024) — EI and Technostress

**Journal:** Business Horizons 67 (2024) 399–409  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.bushor.2024.03.004  
**Citations:** 31  
**Type:** Conceptual — thematic literature review

---

## Summary

Ertiö et al. argue that technostress — the stress employees experience from inability to cope with ICT demands — is an underexamined unintended consequence of digital transformation. Leaders are the key to mitigating it, but traditional leadership skill sets are insufficient. **Emotional intelligence (EI) is the missing conduit**: digital leaders need five specific EI skills to sense and respond to technostressors, deployed through three strategies (communication, transparency, trust). This paper is the clearest evidence in the corpus that EI is a *functional*, *outcome-linked* inner skill — not a soft add-on.

---

## Technostress: The Problem

**Definition** (Brod 1984): stress stemming from inability to cope with ICT use — "a modern disease of adaptation caused by an inability to cope with new computer technologies in a healthy manner."

**Five dimensions** (Ragu-Nathan et al. 2008):

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Techno-overload | Technology forces employees to work faster and longer |
| Techno-invasion | Always reachable; blurred work-life boundaries |
| Techno-complexity | Skills doubt from technology-related demands |
| Techno-insecurity | Job loss threat from automation |
| Techno-uncertainty | Constant upgrades require continuous learning |

Organizational consequences: reduced productivity, lack of innovation, poor job performance, high absenteeism and turnover.

## Five EI Skills for Digital Leaders (Table 1)

| EI Skill | Description | EI Type |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Self-awareness** | Accurately perceiving, understanding, and evaluating one's emotions, thoughts, and behaviors | Individual EI |
| **Self-reflection** | Deep, nonjudgmental self-examination to foster self-awareness and personal growth | Individual EI |
| **Self-regulation** | Managing emotions, impulses, and behaviors in alignment with personal values | Individual EI |
| **Social awareness** | Sensing and understanding others' emotions to navigate the social landscape | Collaborative EI |
| **Empathy** | Capacity to understand and resonate with others' emotions; foster compassion and build relationships | Collaborative EI |

First three skills = **individual EI** (inner dimension). Last two = **relational/collaborative EI**.

## Three EI Strategies for Mitigating Technostress

**1. EI through Communication**
- Open, emotionally intelligent communication across channels
- Active listening; creating safe space for employees to express technology-related concerns
- Communicating the right to disconnect; shielding from techno-overload and techno-invasion
- Requires self-reflection to sense employee needs beyond personal emotional filtering

**2. EI through Transparency**
- Being open about DT changes, especially those causing techno-complexity and techno-uncertainty
- Proactive coping strategies: personalized work plans, team empowerment, continuous learning pathways
- Encouraging ownership rather than reactive coping (venting negative emotions)

**3. EI through Trust**
- Servant leadership model: empowering rather than controlling
- Building social capital — capacity and sustenance of relationships with others
- High authoritative control → resentment, anxiety, performance fatigue; trust → interdependence and productivity
- EI leader is a visionary who inspires confidence and encourages idea sharing

## Key Claims

- **Technostress is an unintended consequence of DT** that technology-focused leaders systematically overlook.
- **Traditional skill sets are insufficient** for digital transformation contexts — leaders must develop new EI competencies.
- **EI has measurable organizational outcomes**: Côté & Miners (2006) found high EI → better mental/physical health, higher job satisfaction, lower turnover.
- EI skills can be deliberately developed through training (Cherniss 2001 — training led to significant improvements in productivity and decreased absenteeism).
- Surveillance/authoritative control approaches backfire: monitored employees more likely to break rules; algorithmic control leads to anxiety and performance fatigue.

## Limitations

- Conceptual only — no empirical data on actual EI skill use by digital leaders.
- Focuses on leaders' EI effects on *employees*, not on leaders' own development trajectory.
- Does not address how EI skills are developed over time.
- Leaders themselves also experience technostress — not addressed.
