# Poláková et al. (2023) — Soft Skills in Industry 5.0

**Journal:** Heliyon 9 (2023) e18670  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e18670  
**Citations:** 218 — highest-cited paper in the entire SLR corpus  
**Type:** Mixed methods — literature review + job market data analysis (19,000 organisations, Slovak job portal, 2017–2021)

---

## Summary

Poláková et al. argue that Industry 5.0's human-centric orientation makes soft skills structurally necessary — not optional add-ons. Combining a literature review (35 papers) with five years of employer job posting data (3,461 distinct soft skills demanded across 19,000 organisations), the paper demonstrates both theoretically and empirically that soft skills consistently constitute ~56% of all employer skill requirements and are growing in importance. The paper provides the macro-level justification for why inner/human capacities are the defining workforce challenge of the current industrial era.

---

## Industry 5.0 vs Industry 4.0

| Dimension | Industry 4.0 | Industry 5.0 |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Focus | Efficiency through technology | Human-centrism + sustainability + resilience |
| Starting question | What can technology do? | What technology should we use? |
| Human role | Being replaced/complemented | Irreplaceable — human judgment, creativity, ethics |
| Skill priority | Digital/technical skills | Balance of soft AND digital skills |

Key shift: Industry 5.0 asks *what technology can we use for human welfare*, not *how do we deploy technology for efficiency*.

## Soft Skills Taxonomy (Table 3)

**Interpersonal skills** (enabling work with others):
- Complex Problem-Solving, Communication, Organisational/Managerial, Teamwork, Leadership, Creativity

**Intrapersonal skills** (cognitive + self-management):
- Analytical & Critical Thinking, Cope with Ambiguity, Value Orientation, Flexibility, Initiative & Engagement, Learning Skills, **Well-being Focused**, Taking Responsibility, **Emotional Intelligence**, Persistence

**Digital skills** (basic and advanced — separate category)

## Empirical Findings (Table 4 — Job Market Data)

Soft skills = consistently **54–57% of all skill requirements** across 2017–2021.

Top demanded soft skills by employers:
1. Communication – interpersonal (~10%)
2. Analytical and Critical Thinking (~7–8%)
3. Problem-solving (~6%)
4. Taking responsibility (~5%)
5. Flexibility (~4–5%)
6. Value orientation (~4%)
7. Well-being focused (~3%)
8. Organisational/Managerial (~3%)

## Key Claims

- Soft skills are **irreplaceable by automation** — machines excel at pattern recognition, data processing, and rule-following; humans remain superior in interpersonal interaction, intuition, and complex contextual decision-making.
- WEF: 85 million jobs disappear by 2025; 97 million new roles emerge — the new roles demand human capacities.
- **Well-being and self-awareness will be critical skills** in navigating unpredictable environments (intrapersonal cluster).
- Balanced proficiency in BOTH soft AND digital skills is required — neither alone is sufficient.
- "CORE competence" = Competence in Organisational and Relational domains — authors propose this acronym to elevate soft skills to STEM-level status.

## Limitations

- Data is from a Slovak job portal — national context; transferability to other markets requires caution.
- Job posting data reflects stated employer preferences, not necessarily actual practice.
- Focuses on workforce broadly, not leadership roles specifically.
- Does not address *how* soft skills are developed — only that they are demanded.

## Key Quotes

> "The human-centric orientation of Industry 5.0 places core human needs and interests at the heart of the production process. Instead of asking what we can do with the new technology, we ask what technology we can use." (p. 2)

> "Well-being and self-awareness will be critical skills in the future because they will help us navigate a changing world of unpredictable environments." (p. 9)

> "Skills and tasks that are not easily replaceable by automation tend to be complemented by automation, whereas social interactions have proven challenging to automate." (p. 7)
